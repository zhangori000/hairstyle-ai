# Gap-fill: hands-on evidence for hairstyle edits and reference transfer

_Second-pass research lane `gap-hair-edit-evidence` (2026-10-20), run with a fresh web-search budget (max 15 searches) to re-check items the first sweep could not verify. Many vendor hosts stayed blocked, so several numbers are still marked unverified._

Long-form notes: [notes/gap-hair-edit-evidence.md](../notes/gap-hair-edit-evidence.md)

## Summary

Gap-fill on identity preservation for hairstyle edits and reference-hair transfer, 2026-10-20. All 15 WebSearch calls were used; every non-GitHub host (arXiv and its mirrors, Arena, Artificial Analysis, vendor docs, blogs, X, Reddit, YouTube, Wayback) is DNS/proxy-blocked, so model-specific evidence is search-snippet-sourced and paper/vendor facts come from GitHub READMEs. Headline: no independent, controlled hairstyle-vs-face-identity test exists for any current model (Nano Banana 2.1/Pro, GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst/Flare, FLUX 3 Image/Klein, Seedream 5.0 Pro, Qwen-Image 3.0/Edit 2511, Krea 3, Ideogram 4.5); what exists is vendor templates, SEO blogs and single-reviewer micro-tests. The only controlled academic result (older FLUX.1 models, arXiv 2608.28802) says identity loss averages ~22-32% across four edit types and that hair edits are the edit type that preserves identity best. Consistent qualitative pattern: Nano Banana has the most hands-on hair evidence (minor facial shifts on single edits, visible drift by ~3-4 chained edits, Pro slightly more faithful); GPT Image 2.5 tops preference boards and has an official ComfyUI hairstyle template but no independent evidence it leaves the face alone (7.7% outside-target pixel change in one review); FLUX 3 is the most conservative editor (0.4% outside-target pixels, drift reduced not eliminated) and FLUX.2-era models tend to keep the source hairstyle under pose gaps; Seedream 5.0 Pro carries a third-party "#1 identity-preserving edit" claim but zero hair tests and ~90 s latency; Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 guides name hair edits as a face-recast failure case; Krea 3 and Ideogram 4.5 are pixel-copy editors with no portrait evidence. Reference transfer ("hair from image 2 on person 1") has no systematic test anywhere; guides converge on front-facing hair-off-face photos, one attribute per pass (length, texture, colour), explicit keep-lists and naming which image controls what, and warn hairline and texture vary. Bald-first is supported by research ablations (HairCLIPv2, HairNet) and every 2025-26 transfer pipeline, with no independent general-editor benchmark. Papers: H-Adapter (ECCV 2026, SNOW Corp, 3.05 s, 72.7% human preference, no code); HairPort (SIGGRAPH 2026, CC BY-NC-ND, >=24 GB VRAM, minutes per pair, Bald Converter + Baldy 6,400 pairs released April 2026, only 2 GitHub issues); DPF (ACM MM 2026 per prior sweep) could not be found by search or GitHub, no code; Stable-Hair v2 is in IEEE TVCG 32(4) 2026 with public code (SD1.5, bald input, no licence). Real-time: Decart's SDKs/pricing (Lucy 2.5 $0.02/s 720p, fast $0.04/s, Lucy Restyle 2 $0.01/s, sub-40 ms vendor claim) and Lucy Edit/VTON docs show no hair capability and warn global edits change identity; StreamDiffusion/StreamDiffusionV2/Daydream Scope are style pipelines with no hair editing. Ranked recommendation: Nano Banana 2.1 default + Pro hero (medium), FLUX 3/Klein for region-locked and self-hosted edits (medium-low), GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst as A/B (medium-low), Seedream 5.0 Pro for reference-transfer experiments only (low), Qwen-Edit-2511 only with identity LoRA (medium), Krea 3/Ideogram 4.5 one bake-off slot (low), HairFastGAN as the only runnable transfer specialist, no real-time vendor (high).

## Findings (17)

- _high confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **No independent controlled test of face identity under hair-only edits exists for any October-2026 model; all model-specific evidence is vendor templates, SEO blogs or single-reviewer micro-tests.**  
  Four extended and several standard searches targeting Nano Banana 2.1/Pro, GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst, FLUX 3/Klein, Seedream 5.0, Qwen-Edit 2511, Krea 3 and Ideogram 4.5 returned only prompt guides, launch posts and vendor pages; each search summary explicitly reported 'no independent test with published results'. The decision must rest on an in-house bake-off (10-20 consented selfies x 10 styles, ArcFace identity + outside-hair-mask SSIM).  
  Sources: <https://www.perfectcorp.com/consumer/blog/hair/i-used-gemini-to-change-my-hairstyle>, <https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/openai/gpt-image-2-5>, <https://www.weshop.ai/blog/seedream-5-0-pro-review-what-early-tests-reveal/>, <https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/krea-3-vs-ideogram-4-5>
- _medium confidence, as of 2026-08._ **The only controlled academic evidence (older FLUX.1 Dev / Kontext-Pro and peers) measures identity loss at roughly 22-32% averaged over four edit types, and hair edits preserve identity best while pose edits preserve it worst.**  
  arXiv 2608.28802 'A Large-scale Evaluation of Text-guided Models for Facial Editing' (Aug 2026) on CelebA and CelebSET; snippet-sourced because arxiv.org is blocked. Implication: hair is the easiest edit class for identity, so a hair-only app should expect better-than-average preservation from any decent editor, and the differentiator is outside-region pixel stability.  
  Sources: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.28802>
- _medium confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **Nano Banana (2.1/Pro) has the most hands-on hairstyle evidence: single edits show occasional minor facial shifts, faces soften and shift after about four chained edits, and Pro keeps facial geometry more consistently than 2.1/2.**  
  Perfect Corp's competitor blog reports realistic hairstyle edits but 'strict face-preservation often requires careful prompt engineering and iterative edits' with 'occasional minor facial shifts'; aichangehair's 2026 multi-round test says drift appears after ~4 back-and-forth edits and recommends a single-step edit from the original; a nine-direction avatar test found Pro held geometry/identity better. A vague two-image prompt let the model replace the hairstyle entirely (kavel.ai). A vendor 92% face-match claim has no methodology.  
  Sources: <https://www.perfectcorp.com/consumer/blog/hair/i-used-gemini-to-change-my-hairstyle>, <https://aichangehair.com/blog/can-nano-banana-change-hairstyle>, <https://app.deskrex.ai/discover/cmi7mj0m0000idbct53dwtmzu>, <https://www.kavel.ai/blog/nano-banana-face-swap-prompt>
- _medium confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst is positioned (by OpenAI partners) as the identity-sensitive variant with an official ComfyUI 'hairstyle and makeup' edit template, but no independent hair-vs-identity measurement exists and the prior sweep's only pixel test found 7.7% of pixels changed outside the target region.**  
  ComfyUI's partner-node docs ship a Sunburst hairstyle/makeup template that names everything to preserve first and claims identity holds across sequential edits (provider claim); Luma recommends Sunburst 'when the person's identity keeps drifting'; gptimage2-5.io proposes a five-round edit test; Picsart advises one change per turn. The 7.7% figure (virse.ai) and the 'regenerates the whole portrait' complaints could not be re-opened. input_fidelity=high is not accepted by 2.5 (prior, SDK-verified).  
  Sources: <https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/partner-nodes/openai/gpt-image-2-5>, <https://comfy.org/workflows/f423e4de5251-f423e4de5251/>, <https://lumalabs.ai/learning-center/articles/gpt-image-2-5-sunburst-and-flare-complete-guide>, <https://www.virse.ai/blog/flux-3-image-review>
- _medium confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **FLUX 3 Image / Klein are the most conservative editors on pixel preservation (0.4% outside-target pixels, 40-round test better than Nano Banana 2.1, 'reduces drift but doesn't eliminate it'), while FLUX.2-era models tend to keep the source hairstyle under reference pose gaps; the Kontext paper itself reports slower face-embedding drift than Gen-4 and GPT-Image-high.**

Klein workflow/prompt guides (floyo, aireiter) say an identity-lock LoRA improves likeness, blurry or shadowed faces drift more, and the original identity reference should be included in every iterative pass. H-Adapter (prior) reports FLUX.2 keeping the source hairstyle when poses differ.  
  Sources: <https://www.virse.ai/blog/flux-3-image-review>, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15742>, <https://floyo.ai/workflows/flux-2-klein-enhancer-for-identity-t-h3558gdqtme5>, <https://aireiter.com/blog/flux-2-klein-prompt-guide-image-editing-consistency>, <https://docs.bfl.ai/guides/usecases_editing_character_consistency>
- _low confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **Seedream 5.0 Pro has a third-party '#1 identity-preserving edit' leaderboard claim but no hands-on hairstyle evidence; one review saw the same face and pose across several prompts with one edit replacing the scene instead; latency ~86-110 s per image.**

No hairstyle evidence exists for hosted Qwen-Image 3.0.**

- _medium confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **Reference-hair transfer ('put the hair from image 2 on the person in image 1') has no systematic test for any current model; guides converge on front-facing hair-off-face photos, one attribute per pass (length, then texture, then colour), explicit keep-lists, and naming which image controls which attribute, and warn that hairline placement and texture vary with the source image.**  
  Media.io, insmind, SeaArt, glbgpt, nanobananaweb and the xmk Seedream reference guide all give the same workflow and caveats; none reports failure rates. Pose gap is the documented hard case in the research literature (H-Adapter, HairPort, Style-Your-Hair); long hair outside the crop and braids/ponytails are documented HairFastGAN failures; colour-vs-cut entanglement is only addressed by HairFastGAN's separate shape/colour references.  
  Sources: <https://www.media.io/image-effects/gemini-hairstyle-prompt.html>, <https://nanobananaweb.com/hairstyle>, <https://www.xmk.com/seedream/blog/seedream-5-0-pro-reference-images>, <https://github.com/deepmancer/HairPort>
- _medium confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **Bald-first (two-step) editing is supported by research ablations and by every 2025-26 transfer pipeline, but there is no independent benchmark of bald-first versus direct editing for general editors.**  
  HairCLIPv2 (ICCV 2023 supplement) ablation: skipping the balding step left visible artifacts, skipping the blending step altered background and identity; HairNet (ECCV 2022) uses baldification as its first stage; Stable-Hair, Stable-Hair v2, HairPort (Bald Converter LoRA + Baldy dataset) and DPF are all bald-then-inject. Consumer tools (BudgetPixel, WeShop, Magic Hour) market bald filters with unverified identity claims. The Gemini 'edit me bald first for updos' tip is prior-sweep anecdote (Tom's Guide, blocked).  
  Sources: <https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/supplemental/Wei_HairCLIPv2_Unifying_Hair_ICCV_2023_supplemental.pdf>, <https://ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/html/5227_ECCV_2022_paper.php>, <https://github.com/deepmancer/HairPort>, <https://github.com/sunkymepro/StableHairV2>
- _medium confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **Chained edits drift on every model: an informal five-edit chain found FLUX Kontext barely drifting, Nano Banana drifting noticeably by edit 3 and GPT-Image-1 by edit 2; a 2026 benchmark paper confirms subtle facial changes accumulate across rounds.**  
  aiofm.info's chain test has no sample size or metric and used GPT-Image-1; arXiv 2609.04151 'Persistent Identity Preservation in Generative Image Models' shows GPT-IMAGE-2 in its drift-trajectory figure (numbers not captured). Product rule supported by all sources: re-edit from the original, keep the original as a reference in every pass, never iterate on an output more than once or twice.  
  Sources: <https://aiofm.info/en/guides/image-editing-models-2026>, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04151>, <https://aichangehair.com/blog/can-nano-banana-change-hairstyle>
- _medium confidence, as of 2026-10-20._ **H-Adapter (arXiv 2606.25578) is 'Pose-Robust Hairstyle Transfer via Attention-Derived, Source-Aligned Hair Masks' by Seulgi Jeong, Yunseong Cho and Sanghun Park (SNOW Corp), posted 24 Jun 2026, listed as ECCV 2026; reports best FID/FID_CLIP/CLIP-I under pose differences, FID 12.47 (CelebA-HQ pose-different) / 11.56 (pose-agnostic), 3.05 s runtime, 72.7% human preference (p<0.001); no code (project page only; GitHub search 0 repos on 2026-10-20).**  
  Numbers come from aggregator snippets (emergentmind/chatpaper) because arXiv and mirrors are blocked; the prior sweep's pairwise 81.1/72.9/55.3% figures may be the per-baseline breakdown of the same study and are not reconciled. Adds a VLM-as-a-judge protocol, prompt-based hair colour control and IP-Adapter compatibility.
