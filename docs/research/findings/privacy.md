## Summary

A hairstyle try-on is not facial recognition, but it is not automatically outside biometric law either. Illinois BIPA defines a biometric identifier as a "scan of hand or face geometry" with no identification-purpose requirement, excludes photographs but not geometry derived from them, and plaintiffs have sued virtual try-on tools on that theory; eyewear cases were dismissed only because of BIPA's health-care exemption, which a hair app cannot use, and a cosmetics try-on case (MAC) survived dismissal in June 2026. The 2024 amendment (P.A. 103-0769, effective Aug 2, 2024; held retroactive by the Seventh Circuit Apr 1, 2026) caps recovery at one per person per method and lets a "written release" be an electronic signature, so BIPA-grade consent is cheap: a pre-camera notice stating purpose and retention, an affirmative e-signature, a published retention schedule, no sale, vendor-disclosure consent, and never persisting landmarks. Texas CUBI, Washington (RCW 19.375 and the My Health My Data Act, which lists biometric data as consumer health data with a private right of action and no revenue floor) and Colorado's biometric amendment (any amount of biometric data triggers duties; 24-month destruction clock) follow the same notice-consent-destroy model. Under GDPR/UK GDPR a face photo is personal data but only becomes Article 9 biometric data when processed by specific technical means for unique identification (Recital 51, EDPB 3/2019, ICO), so on-device landmarks for alignment are not special-category data; consent plus a light DPIA and vendor DPAs is the clean path. The EU AI Act's Article 50 applies from Aug 2, 2026: the model vendor must machine-readably mark outputs, you must disclose AI-edited images to users, and purely personal use by your friends is outside the deployer duty.

Provider choice is the biggest lever. Verified/mirrored defaults: Anthropic (no training, DPA, ~30-day retention, ZDR for approved orgs); Gemini unpaid tier trains on and human-reviews inputs and bans under-18 audiences, paid tier logs ~55 days; Vertex AI gives contractual no-training, a 24-hour cache you can disable, and region pinning; OpenAI keeps 30-day abuse logs, ZDR only via sales, and retains CSAM-flagged image inputs even under ZDR; Replicate deletes API predictions after 1 hour; fal keeps request JSON 30 days unless you send X-Fal-Store-IO: 0 and serves outputs on a public-by-URL CDN unless you set an ACL; Black Forest Labs' direct API and Stability train by default. Both app stores now require explicit, in-app consent before sending photos to third-party AI, push the OS photo picker over broad photo permissions, and require report/flag plus NCII/deepfake prevention for AI image apps. Recommended posture: device-only detection and cropping, ephemeral cloud rendering with EXIF stripped and no first-party storage, results kept on device, and an opt-in encrypted library with auto-expiry and per-user deletion.

## Recommendations

- Adopt a three-tier posture: Tier 0 device-only detection/cropping/EXIF stripping for everything before consent; Tier 1 ephemeral cloud rendering (no first-party storage, results on device) as the default; Tier 2 encrypted, auto-expiring library only as an explicit opt-in.
- Ship a BIPA-grade 'Face data notice' before the camera opens: purpose, what is processed (photo, on-device landmarks), where it goes (named vendors), retention ('relay keeps nothing; vendor abuse logs up to N days; results stay on your device'), no sale/ads/identification/training, withdrawal path, and an affirmative checkbox or signature as the electronic-signature written release; store a hashed, versioned consent record.
- Publish a retention and destruction schedule (BIPA §15(a), Colorado) and make 'Delete everything' (device cache, server records, vendor deletions where APIs exist) and account deletion (in-app plus web URL for Play) first-class features.
- Choose vendors by data posture: Anthropic for analysis/suggestions; Vertex AI (cache disabled, region pinned) or OpenAI (ask for ZDR; otherwise promise 30 days) for rendering; Replicate or fal (X-Fal-Store-IO: 0, sync_mode/data URIs, private ACLs) for hosted open models; never the Gemini unpaid tier, BFL direct API without opt-out, or Stability without opt-out; prefer self-hosting FLUX.1 [schnell] if GPU is available.
- Treat the on-device landmark pass as a 'scan of face geometry' for BIPA purposes: never persist or transmit landmarks or templates, say so in the notice, and keep raw photos out of your own storage entirely in Tier 1.
- Gate cloud features to self-declared adults (Gemini API bans under-18 audiences; COPPA), and do not implement face-based age estimation or any inference of protected attributes (EU AI Act biometric categorisation; Anthropic AUP).
- Design out non-consensual editing: default to live selfie capture, single-face only, attestation for gallery uploads of other people, hair-only crop of reference photos on device before upload, no sharing to other users without the subject's own account, and an in-app report/flag control (required by Google Play's AI-Generated Content policy and Apple 1.2).
- Constrain generation to hair attributes with a typed prompt builder rather than free text, run NSFW checks on inputs and outputs, and keep a written record of safety prompts/edge cases tested plus a full-access reviewer test account (reported Aug 2026 Play enforcement).
- Keep provider SynthID/C2PA marks intact, re-sign exports with C2PA after any re-encode, and show a visible 'AI-edited preview' label on results and stylist exports (EU AI Act Art. 50(4), Pennsylvania notice defence, store policies).
- Use the OS photo picker (PHPicker / Android Photo Picker) instead of broad photo permissions, declare Photos as collected for App Functionality (not linked, not tracking) on both store labels, name each AI vendor in the disclosure, and avoid analytics/ads SDKs in the camera flow.
- Engineer the photo lifecycle as a typed state machine (Picked -> Prepared -> ConsentChecked -> Sent -> Received -> Discarded) whose only non-user terminal transition is Discarded; log ids and sizes never bytes; add a test that asserts no event or log can carry image data.
- Maintain docs/privacy/providers.md with dated verbatim quotes of each vendor's training/retention clauses and re-check them before each release; sign DPAs (Anthropic DPA is public; Google Cloud and OpenAI DPAs; fal DPA for enterprise-ready models) and use SCCs for EU users.
- Do a lightweight DPIA now (biometric imagery, innovative tech, third-party AI) and keep it in the repo; it doubles as the design rationale for the consent and retention spec.
