_2026-10-20. Research only; no app code exists. Every price and model name carries an as-of date, and "verified" means read on a primary vendor page during this work. "Secondary" means a mirror, snippet or third-party table. "Unverified" means nobody could reach the source from the sandbox. Re-check anything secondary or unverified on the live page before it goes into a budget, a consent screen or a vendor decision. Supporting evidence lives next to this file: `findings/` (one document per research lane with fact-check verdicts), `notes/` (raw notes with every URL), `prototypes/` (scripts, measurements, images) and `planning/` (the three competing plans, the judges' scores, the agent-drafted synthesis and the critic's review this plan was edited against)._

## 0. The short version

Build a private web app, opened from a link, that does one loop well: capture a photo with on-device guidance, understand the face and current hair, propose five ideas that fit, render the top three on the user's own face, let the user refine with a typed vocabulary, and export a reference sheet a stylist can act on. Keep every face byte off your own servers. Spend about $0.70 per session at list prices, which is $20 to $30 a month for ten friends.

Direct answers to the questions in the brief:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| State machines? | Yes, three small ones in plain TypeScript (session flow, photo lifecycle, render job), each a discriminated union with a pure exhaustive reducer and table tests. No XState until a parallel region appears; never XState 6 (alpha). |
| Good typing? | Yes, and further than usual: strict compiler flags, Zod 4 at every boundary, branded ids, and a `PhotoBytes` type that can only exist inside the privacy module, enforced by a type test so no log, event or database row can ever carry image data. |
| "Zero comments for AI"? | Half right. Ban comments that restate code, names or types, and ban commented-out code. Keep four tagged comment classes: `why:` (link an ADR), `invariant:`, `privacy:` (already mandated by CLAUDE.md) and `quirk:` for gotchas an assistant cannot infer. Put navigation knowledge in a short CLAUDE.md, ADRs and tests, not in prose comments. |
| Event-driven architecture? | No. At friends scale the workload is two to six provider calls per screen with one consumer. One route handler per candidate, a `renders` table, and an append-only events table (ids, sizes, costs, outcomes, never bytes) give you "events as data" without a bus, CQRS, event sourcing or Temporal. The trigger for adding one durable runner is measured, not assumed. |
| Image analysis | Split geometry from semantics. On-device MediaPipe gives landmarks, head pose, a hair mask and a crop for free and privately (prototyped, matches Google's golden assets). Face-shape *labels* from landmark ratios are weak (prototyped, between-face spread is tiny and labels flip with pose and crop), so treat shape as soft ratios and let a vision model and the user confirm. A vision LLM describes hair, flags bad photos, writes generator-ready edit instructions and stylist text well (prototyped). |
| Calling AI | One provider interface for rendering with capability flags, typed refusal outcomes and a fallback chain; structured outputs with a strict schema for analysis; a typed model registry with prices, retention classes and shutdown dates, and a test that fails once a model is retired. Analysis costs cents; rendering is the whole bill. |
| Pricing | Nano Banana 2.1 renders cost $0.034 per 1K image (verified); a full session is about $0.65 to $0.75; ten friends cost $20 to $30 a month in AI plus $0 hosting on free tiers. Incumbents charge $5 to $25 a month or $9 to $17 one-time. |
| Product features | Incumbents are catalog-first (pick a preset, pay); complaints are paywalls on saving, billing friction, an uncanny "synthetic mask" look and poor textured-hair results. The gap this app can own: suitability first with rendering as proof, a typed refine loop, a stylist handoff with a reply link, honest coverage of textured hair and men's medium or long cuts, and friends who never pay. |
| "Upload someone's hair and put it on me" | Feasible on a still photo with today's multi-image editors, quality unproven: no systematic test exists for any 2026 model, and this work did not run one either. Ship it in phase 3 behind a flag as "inspired by this hair", with the reference cropped to hair on device so the other person's face never uploads. |
| "Turn on video and put the hair on me" | Live photoreal haircut replacement was not found in any product or model (vendor sites were partly unreachable, so "not found" rather than "does not exist"). What works: live on-device hair recolor from the MediaPipe mask (prototyped at 8 to 12 fps on CPU), freeze-frame-to-render, and a short turntable clip generated from the chosen still. |

### What was actually prototyped, and what was not

Run: MediaPipe face landmarks, hair segmentation and multiclass segmentation in Python on 36 public images (40 faces) against Google's golden assets, with a rule-based face-shape classifier and repeatability tests; the same models in headless Chromium from a real `getUserMedia` stream (fake camera) with a Web Worker and the GPU delegate on software GL; and a vision-model analysis-and-suggestion loop on five photos, run twice for consistency, using the research model's own vision rather than an API call.

Not run: any generative hair render (no image-generation keys and the vendor hosts were blocked), any live API call to Google, OpenAI, Anthropic or BFL, any two-image reference transfer, Safari or a real phone GPU, and any dark-skin textured-hair subject (no suitable public image was reachable). Those are the first paid experiments in the plan, and they cost under $10 in total.

## 1. Thesis and positioning

Position the app as "find what suits my face, prove it on my own photo, then hand it to a stylist". The market default is upload, pick from 50 to 250 presets, pay. No shipped product was found that combines a typed refine vocabulary, a suitability-first flow with rendering as proof, and a stylist handoff with a reply link. That claim is narrower than "nobody does any of this": 2026 newcomers are moving to prompt-driven and reference-driven try-on and to analysis-first flows (Hairu, HairTry, HairWow, Hiface, CutMuse), and the barber reply link already exists in an open-source hackathon project, HairPath/ManeRoute, which this plan copies with credit.

Three evidence facts drive every choice:

1. No independent, controlled test of face identity under hair-only edits exists for any October 2026 editor. Friends' consented photos and a blind A/B are the only decision tool you will have.
2. The edit model is the only cost that matters. Analysis is cents per photo on any model.
3. Live photoreal haircut replacement was not found in 2026, so "live" means on-device recolor, labelled as a preview.

## 2. MVP scope and what is deferred

**In the MVP:** a web PWA opened from a link (iOS Safari and Android Chrome); invite-only magic-link login with an adults-only self-declaration; a layered face-data consent before the camera; file-input capture with HEIC decoded on device, EXIF stripped and a 2048 px cap; on-device MediaPipe gate, head-and-shoulders crop, hair mask and current-hair descriptors; one Claude Sonnet 5.5 structured-output analysis into a typed, user-confirmed face-and-hair profile; five ideas with taxonomy ids and plain "why it suits" reasons; the top three rendered on the user's face (two candidates each) with Nano Banana 2.1 on Vertex AI; an on-device candidate pre-gate and pixel paste-back outside the hair region; refine chips and free text mapped to one `RefinementIntent` enum, always re-rendered from the original photo with the accepted candidate as a reference; a hero render on Nano Banana Pro; an export sheet (hero, before, stylist script, current-hair description, maintenance, a visible "AI-edited preview" label) shared through the user's own share sheet; a stylist reply link that carries only a token and the typed ask; an "I got the cut" before-and-after compare kept on device; delete-everything; per-user caps, a kill switch, a cost ledger, a feedback table, and tracing with inputs and outputs switched off.

**Deferred:** native or Expo; live video of any kind (recolor preview is a phase 3 experiment); reference-hair transfer (phase 3); multi-angle or turntable output; self-hosted models or any GPU; a durable job runner, event bus, CQRS or Temporal; server-side photo or render storage (an opt-in encrypted library is an owner decision); OpenAI as a default renderer; payments, public sharing, feeds, anyone under 18; hard face-shape labels or attractiveness scores; hair-specific vendor APIs (YouCam) and the BFL direct API; C2PA re-signing infrastructure.

## 3. Roadmap

**Phase 0, walking skeleton (weeks 1 and 2).** Next.js App Router, strict TypeScript, Zod 4.6.7, t3-env, better-auth 1.7.12 magic link with an allowlist; a GCP project with billing, the Vertex cache disabled, the region decision recorded and the abuse-monitoring exception requested; an Anthropic spend limit and the DPA; the consent screen with a hashed, versioned record; `<input type=file accept=image/* capture=user>` decoded with `createImageBitmap` (HEIC is first-class: Safari 27.0, released 2026-09-14, stopped converting HEIC to JPEG on upload) into an `OffscreenCanvas` and out as JPEG; one Sonnet 5.5 analysis; one Nano Banana 2.1 render with a fixed "edit only the hair, keep everything else identical" template; the result in IndexedDB; delete-everything. *Exit:* three friends render a style on their own face from a link on iOS 27 Safari and Android Chrome; a type test proves no log, event, trace or database row can carry image bytes; measured per-render cost, p50 and p95 latency, refusal count and backgrounded-tab loss are recorded (no source published Nano Banana latency, so these measurements set the phase 1 bars); the Vercel body and duration limits are confirmed empirically or Cloudflare is chosen instead.

**Phase 1, suggest, render, refine (weeks 3 to 6).** MediaPipe tasks-vision 1.1.1 in a classic Web Worker: the gate (one face, yaw and pitch under 15 degrees, face at least 15% of the frame, eyes open, hair inside the frame), the crop, and the hair mask as the dilated union of the hair segmenter and the multiclass segmenter's hair class run once on the still; a `getUserMedia` viewfinder (oval overlay, lighting meter, auto-capture) behind a flag with file input as the fallback, tested in iOS 27 home-screen mode; catalog v1 with taxonomy ids (LLM-drafted from the open seed repos in the appendix, human-reviewed, Zod-validated in CI); the profile confirm screen; the top three rendered with a progressive reveal; the pre-gate and paste-back; refine with the accepted candidate as a reference; colour and beard as separate passes; caps, kill switch and tracing. *Exit:* ten friends onboarded; at least 60% of sessions reach an accepted look within three refine turns; at least 80% "still me" thumbs; two independent analysis calls agree on at least 80% of enum fields and four of six taxonomy ids; the refusal fallback has been exercised.

**Phase 2, handoff, harness, bake-off (weeks 7 to 10).** The export sheet and the stylist reply link; "I got the cut" compare; a golden set frozen with hashes; the offline harness; a two-ring bake-off (friends' photos go only to Vertex, Anthropic and, for opt-ins, OpenAI; synthetic SFHQ faces go to FLUX 3 via fal, Klein, Seedream and one of Krea 3 or Ideogram 4.5); gpt-image-2.5-sunburst wired as the opt-in fallback; renderer and prompt frozen in an ADR with numbers; the DPIA and `docs/privacy/providers.md`; a paid stylist review of the catalog if nobody in the group is one. *Exit:* two real salon visits used the sheet; the blind A/B scores at least 70% "still me" and 60% "the style I asked for", with the textured-hair subset reported separately; p95 time to first candidate within the phase 0 bar.

**Phase 3, time-boxed experiments (weeks 11 to 15).** Reference-hair "inspired by this hair"; on-device recolor preview; an uploaded-selfie-video edit experiment; friends' polls via the share sheet; passkeys; the opt-in library if you want it; a durable runner only if measurement demands it. *Exit:* each ships behind a flag only if it passes the phase 2 bars on at least 20 pairs; otherwise it is written up and shelved.

**Ongoing:** quarterly re-read of vendor clauses; the Anthropic usage-policy revision check on 2026-11-12; the model-shutdown test (gpt-image-1 retired 2026-10-23, gpt-image-1.5 and -mini on 2026-12-01); the harness re-run on every prompt or model change.

## 4. Product and UX flow

**Capture.** Good and bad example photos; the OS photo picker, never library permission; the gate asks for a retake instead of guessing (two of the five prototype photos deserved one); an optional hair-down or side shot when the hairline is hidden; "remove hat and glasses" when the multiclass "others" class fires. The oval overlay, lighting meter and auto-capture ship only with the viewfinder flag, because the native camera UI opened by a file input cannot host an overlay. Mobbin references: Noom's live lighting meter, MoonPay's auto-capture, Warby Parker's "no face detected" toast.

**Consent.** Layered: a short notice (what happens, who receives the crop, "we keep nothing on our servers", delete anytime) with "learn more" opening the full face-data notice: purpose; on-device landmarks never stored or sent; named recipients (Anthropic for description and preview checks; Google Cloud Vertex AI for rendering; OpenAI only if the user switches on the fallback); retention in plain words with the day counts filled in only from primary pages; no sale, ads, identification or training; an affirmative checkbox stored as a hashed, versioned record next to the notice text; adults only; re-consent when the vendor list changes. One optional toggle: "allow a second renderer (OpenAI) if Google declines". Mobbin references: Wise, Noom and Snapchat "My Selfie" consent copy; Tinder's "everything stays on your device" phrasing.

**Profile and ideas.** The user confirms every field: picture-based curl pattern (1A to 4C plus mixed), stretched length, undertone swatch (photo undertone is unreliable), style lane. Face shape reads "between oval and oblong", never a hard class (labels flipped across crops in the prototype). Five ideas with boldness 1 to 5, styling minutes, maintenance, grow-out and a balance-language "why" (adds height, softens the jaw); never a score.

**Render and refine.** The top three ideas render on the user's face with a progressive reveal over the source photo (Grok's blurred reveal pattern); before/after slider; the other two ideas one tap away. Chips ("shorter", "more curl", "less side volume", "5-minute version", "keep the cut, change the colour", "what would a stylist say") and free text parse into one `RefinementIntent`; one attribute per pass; "no change, that contradicts" is a legal answer; every refine re-renders from the original with the accepted candidate attached as a reference, so the spec converges and pixels never chain. Mobbin references: Zillow's slider plus style rail plus thumbs; Google Lens's follow-up chips.

**Handoff and return.** A one-tap sheet with the hero, the before, a three-line ask in stylist vocabulary (guard numbers, lengths in cm), the current-hair description, maintenance and the visible label; the user shares it through the share sheet; the reply link (Can do / Let's talk / Not yet, after HairPath/ManeRoute) carries only a token and the typed ask, never an image. After the appointment, "I got the cut" compares the real result on device and tags what differed: the natural return visit and the only ground truth on achievability.

**Friends.** Allowlist invites; phase 3 poll links carry votes only, images travel by share sheet (Alta's friends feed is the reference, without the feed). No public feed.

**Parked ideas (with the reason):** a Krea-style resemblance knob (identity versus style strength) once the pre-gate exposes a measurable score; Warby Parker's "erase your glasses" as "hide current hair or hat" (needs a mask-aware bald-first pass, phase 3); per-style thumbnails rendered on the user's own face for the whole catalog (Linktree's pattern; cost: $0.034 per thumbnail); a LivePortrait-style animated preview of the chosen still (MIT code, but the bundled InsightFace models are non-commercial); seasonal trend refresh from Pinterest Predicts and Google Trends rather than the LLM; per-user LoRA identity locking on an open editor (Qwen-Image-Edit) if self-hosting ever happens.

## 5. AI pipeline and provider choices

Prices as of 2026-10-20. Full tables with sources: `findings/gap-edit-pricing-policies.md`, `findings/pricing.md`.

**On-device (tier 0).** `@mediapipe/tasks-vision` 1.1.1 (Apache-2.0), self-hosted wasm (13.5 MB), landmarker (3.76 MB), hair segmenter (0.78 MB), multiclass (16.4 MB, lazy-loaded, once per still) in a classic Web Worker (module workers fail to load the bundle). Measured in the prototype: still analysis about 150 ms; landmarks 25 to 34 ms per frame in a worker; hair mask 8 to 12 fps on CPU wasm; GPU delegate unmeasured on phones. Outputs: gate verdict, crop, hair mask, coarse descriptors (length class, bangs, volume, coarse colour), soft geometry ratios as prompt text. Known gaps: the hair segmenter fires on fur and fabric (gate it on "face found and the mask component touches the head"), is weak on grey hair over light backgrounds (hence the union with the multiclass mask), and the multiclass model scores 71.9 mean IoU on Monk tones 9 and 10 against 81.7 on tones 1 and 2 (model card). The gate, mask and crop path is untested on 3C to 4C hair and dark skin until the first consented photos arrive.

**Analysis and suggestion.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 with structured outputs for analyze (image sent once) and suggest (text only); Claude Haiku 5.5 for refine deltas and the candidate judge. Neither is a "Covered Model": image inputs are ephemeral and conversation content is not retained by default (platform docs read 2026-10-20; the separate commercial retention article was unreachable, so re-read it before the consent copy is final). Fable 5.1 and Mythos carry mandatory 30-day retention and are avoided. About $0.014 per 1024 px photo analysis on Sonnet at verified rates, a floor that excludes thinking tokens, the system prompt and caching; the prototype's all-Sonnet session estimate was $0.12. Schema rules for Claude structured outputs: flat, all-required, `additionalProperties: false`, no numeric min or max, `unknown` and `not_visible` enum members, per-field confidence and provenance (measured, model or user), raw text persisted, your own validate-and-retry loop. Guardrails: never identify; never infer gender, age, ethnicity, emotion or attractiveness (child: refuse); the user chooses the style lane; a stylist-reviewed bank for coily and curly hair; one image-left/right convention.

Gemini image models accept multiple reference images (14 verified for Nano Banana 2 and Pro, 6 at high fidelity; the limit for 2.1 is unverified) and take no mask input (the SDK's image config has none). All Gemini outputs carry SynthID.

**Interface.** `HairEditProvider.render({ original, hairSpec, keepList, instruction, priorAccepted?, referenceHairCrop?, mask?, boxes?, size })` returning `{ image, providerMeta, safetyOutcome, measuredCost, latencyMs }`, with capability flags (`maxReferenceImages`, `supportsMask`, `supportsBoxes`, `retentionClass`, `residency`) enforced in your own code. Vercel AI SDK 7 (ai 7.0.146) now documents `generateImage` with `prompt.images` and `prompt.mask` and per-model `supportsFileInputs` and `supportsMaskInputs` flags, but it does not reject unsupported inputs, so a mask sent to Gemini is silently ignored. Mask-constrained providers must get a mask dilated well beyond the current hair, because an edit cannot grow hair outside its mask. Model ids, prices, retention classes and shutdown dates live in one typed config with as-of dates and a test that fails past a shutdown date.

**Candidates, gate, paste-back.** Two candidates per look; a bald-first two-step chain replaces one candidate when the target is shorter or tighter (two calls, costed). On device: one face found; outside-hair-mask SSIM against the original above a floor calibrated offline on the golden set (landmark displacement only as a secondary signal, because the mesh is template-dominated); hair-region change above a no-op floor; then original pixels composited back outside the feathered, dilated union of the original hair mask and a hair mask computed on the output. Phase 2 adds the Haiku 5.5 rubric (instruction following, over-editing, naturalness, artifacts, 0 to 10, threshold 6); the consent names Anthropic as receiving previews for that check. ArcFace stays in the offline harness: InsightFace weights are non-commercial, and a runtime one-to-one comparison would be biometric verification.

**Refusals.** `IMAGE_SAFETY` and `PROHIBITED_CONTENT` (Google) and `moderation_blocked` (OpenAI) become a typed `SafetyOutcome`; chain Nano Banana 2.1, then Pro, then gpt-image-2.5 (opt-ins only), then an honest decline with retake tips; track the refusal rate per provider. Enforce one render id per request in your `renders` table so a retry never double-bills (YouCam documents that retries re-charge; the Google and OpenAI behaviour was not checked).

## 6. Stretch features

**Reference-hair transfer ("her hair on me").** Tier 1 (still photo): feasible, quality unproven. Multi-image prompting exists on Gemini image models, GPT Image 2.5 (up to 16 inputs) and FLUX 3 (up to 10 references), and guides converge on the same recipe: front-facing, hair-off-face photos; one attribute per pass (length, then texture, then colour); explicit keep-lists; naming which image controls which attribute. They also warn that hairline placement and texture vary with the source. No systematic test exists for any current model, and this work did not run a two-image prompt either; a 10-pair synthetic test on Nano Banana 2.1 costs under $1 and is the first thing to do in phase 3. Shape: crop the reference to hair on device (the other person's face never uploads), have the vision model write a hair spec from it, send user crop plus hair crop plus spec, bald-first for tighter targets, label it "inspired by this hair", expect braids, updos and pose gaps to fail. Specialists: HairFastGAN (MIT code, sub-second, but non-commercial StyleGAN2 weights and aligned crops only) is the only fast, permissively licensed transfer model and is a ring B second opinion; Stable-Hair v1 (Apache-2.0) and v2 (public code, TVCG 2026, bald input, no licence stated) are heavier runnable options; HairPort (SIGGRAPH 2026, CC BY-NC-ND, 24 GB VRAM, minutes per pair) is research-only; H-Adapter (ECCV 2026) and DPF have no code. Tier 2 (multi-angle stills): feasible but inconsistent; only with user-supplied side photos edited with the same spec. Verdict: phase 3 experiment, shipped behind a flag if at least 60% of 20 or more pairs are rated "recognisably that hair" and pass the identity bars.

**Live video.** Tier 4 (live photoreal cut): not found in 2026. YouCam's live mode is preset AR overlays; Snapchat lenses do colour; Decart's real-time models (Lucy, $0.02 per second, sub-40 ms vendor claim, unverified) document no hair capability and warn that global edits change identity; YouCam's API has no hair video endpoint. Snapchat, TikTok and Perfect Corp sites were unreachable, so this is "none found", not proof of absence. Tier 3 (4 to 8 s turntable from the chosen still via Veo 3.1 Lite or Fast, $0.15 to $0.60 per clip, secondary): feasible, but it sends the render to another vendor, so off by default. A third option, editing a short uploaded selfie video with Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 (up to 10 s input, not available in the EEA, UK or Switzerland, untested for hair), is a phase 3 experiment with the same privacy cost. 3D hair-asset lenses via Snap Camera Kit were rejected: Snap approval, online-only resolution, Snap branding, and low realism. What ships: on-device hair recolor from the MediaPipe mask (8 to 12 fps CPU floor; measure the GPU delegate on phones; check the iOS 26.x home-screen camera rotation bug on iOS 27) plus freeze-frame-to-render, labelled "preview". The server API stays client-agnostic so a native shell could reuse it if live ever becomes the core.

## 7. Architecture and engineering practices

**Platform.** Next.js App Router PWA, TypeScript, on Vercel Hobby (the plan page says personal and non-commercial; a zero-revenue friends app is not addressed, so Cloudflare Workers is the drop-in if the terms or the limits bite); every vendor call goes through a route handler; no native app. Web wins on distribution: TestFlight limits (100 internal, 10,000 external testers, 90-day builds) and the $99 per year program only buy you live-video capability that does not exist yet.

**State machines: yes, three small ones, no framework.** (1) A client `Session` as one discriminated union (idle, consented, captured, gated ok or retake, analyzing, confirming, suggesting, rendering, reviewing, refining, accepted, exported, plus errored) with a pure exhaustive `(state, event) => state` reducer and table tests. (2) The photo lifecycle: Picked, Prepared, ConsentChecked, Sent, Received, Discarded; Discarded is the only non-user terminal; bytes exist only between Prepared and Received; a test asserts no event carries bytes. (3) The server render job: queued, running, succeeded, failed, refused, cancelled, as a guarded transition table shared as vocabulary. Promote to XState 5.33.3 only if parallel regions appear (a viewfinder beside an upload); never XState 6 (6.0.0-alpha.70 as of 2026-10-19). This matches the React docs' advice to replace boolean flags with one status value and Effective TypeScript's items on unions of interfaces, exhaustiveness with `never`, and brands.

**Typing.** `strict`, `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`, `exactOptionalPropertyTypes`; ts-reset; t3-env; Zod 4 at every boundary with types inferred from schemas; branded ids (`UserId`, `SessionId`, `PhotoId`, `RenderId`, `ConsentVersion`, `TaxonomyId`) and a `PhotoBytes` type that can exist only inside `lib/privacy` and never in log, event or database schemas (type test); exhaustive-switch lint; versioned schemas; taxonomy ids instead of names; `RefinementIntent` and `SafetyOutcome` as enums; geometry ratios bucketed into enums before storage.

**Async jobs: none in the MVP.** One route handler per candidate (not one handler fanning out), progress over SSE, a `renders` row per call for idempotency, status, measured cost and outcome; Screen Wake Lock while rendering; a backgrounded tab may lose one render of about $0.04, which the client re-requests under a new id, and that loss rate is measured. If it exceeds a few percent, or a provider exceeds the function limit, add one durable runner (Trigger.dev, 20 concurrent on the free plan, verified; Inngest's free plan allows 5 concurrent steps, which would queue a demo evening) whose payloads carry keys into a private bucket with a 24-hour lifecycle and delete-on-completion, never bytes, and name that processor in the consent. No event bus, CQRS, event sourcing or Temporal.

**"Zero comments" should mean** none that restate code, types or names, and no commented-out code; keep four tagged classes: `why:` (link the ADR), `invariant:`, `privacy:` (CLAUDE.md already mandates these wherever photos are captured, stored or transmitted) and `quirk:` for gotchas an assistant cannot infer (classic worker, not module worker; the GPU delegate needs the task's own canvas; `createImageBitmap` decodes HEIC on Safari; the iOS rotation bug). No TODO without an issue link. This follows Anthropic's own guidance that CLAUDE.md should hold only what the assistant would otherwise get wrong, that hooks are deterministic while prose is advisory, and that tests and runnable checks are the spec; Google's TypeScript style guide says the same about comments that restate names or types.

**Repo conventions for AI-assisted coding.** CLAUDE.md of at most 60 lines holding only non-derivable rules; an AGENTS.md alias; `docs/decisions` ADRs, each with a "what would change this" line; `docs/privacy/data-flow.md` and `providers.md` with dated verbatim vendor clauses; `.claude/rules` scoped to `lib/privacy/**` and `lib/providers/**`; hooks that run typecheck, lint and tests after edits; a code review pass and a privacy checklist before every PR; small kebab-case modules with colocated `*.test.ts` and `*.test-d.ts`; bug fixes land with a failing-then-passing test; an eslint ban on `console.*` in `lib/privacy`; prompts and schemas as versioned files with golden outputs; a prompt change is a PR that shows harness deltas; `.env.example` committed; one commit-trailer policy decided in week 1.

**Observability.** `@ai-sdk/otel` to Langfuse Cloud Hobby (free, 50k units per month, 2 users, verified) with `recordInputs` and `recordOutputs` false; traces carry ids, model ids, tokens, cost, latency and safety outcome; a feedback table (thumbs, reason, failure tag) from day one; evals in earnest after about 50 real sessions, the bake-off before that.

**Spend caps.** A Vertex budget and quota; the Anthropic console limit (Start tier $500 per month cap, verified); an OpenAI project budget (Build tier $500 per month, secondary); a per-user daily cap of 6 renders and a per-session cap in the database (ten friends at the daily cap cannot exceed about $70 a month); render ids enforced server-side; a global `KILL_SWITCH` env; an Upstash rate limit by user; alerts at $25 and $35 of a $40 monthly budget. The alerts and the kill switch are the budget guard; the per-user cap only bounds the worst case. Stagger demo evenings: Gemini's reported $10 per 10 minutes spend rate and about 20 images per minute on the first tier are secondary.

## 8. Privacy and legal posture

**Tiers.** Tier 0 on device (decode, EXIF strip, gate, crop, landmarks, masks, descriptors) before consent; tier 1 ephemeral cloud by default (cropped JPEG inline base64 to a route handler in memory, to the vendor, back to the browser; no bucket, no first-party storage; only profile text, consent, feedback and cost rows persist); tier 2 opt-in encrypted auto-expiring library deferred to an owner decision. Never the Gemini free tier (it trains on and human-reviews inputs); billing attached before the first photo. Landmarks count as biometric personal data under the ICO reading (algorithmic feature extraction) but not Article 9 special-category data (not for identification); never persist or transmit them. The confirmed profile stores bucketed enums, not raw ratios, and sits inside the published retention schedule and delete-everything; delete-everything keeps the hashed consent proof and a deletion record.

**Law.** Illinois BIPA reaches try-on "scans": the MAC Cosmetics motion to dismiss was denied in June 2026 in the Northern District of Illinois (trade-press and law-firm accounts; the opinion was unreachable), and the statute's "private entity" covers individuals, so a hobbyist is covered (statutory reading, not sourced to the mirror used here). P.A. 103-769 (effective 2024-08-02) makes an electronic signature a written release and caps recovery at one per person per collection method, applied retroactively in Gregg v. Central Transport (7th Cir., 2026-04-01). The same consent fits Washington's My Health My Data Act (biometric data listed as consumer health data, no revenue floor, private right of action) and Colorado (biometric duties since 2025-07-01; photographs excluded unless used for identification). GDPR and UK GDPR: the household exemption is unlikely to cover a service run for others with third-party processors, so publish an Article 13 notice, keep a light DPIA in the repo, execute the Anthropic DPA and Google Cloud's data processing terms, and use SCCs for EU or UK friends. The EU AI Act's Article 50 applies since 2026-08-02; the Digital Omnibus grandfathers only the 50(2) marking duty until 2026-12-02; friends' purely personal use is outside 50(4), but "face replacement or substantial facial modification" is a listed marking example and stylist exports are professional-facing, so every result carries a visible "AI-edited preview" label. Provider marks (SynthID, C2PA) survive only when the export is the provider's unmodified bytes; paste-back composites lose C2PA and rely on the label; no re-signing infrastructure is built. TAKE IT DOWN: an invite-only app very likely is not a "covered platform" (it must "serve the public" and primarily host user content); implement report, flag and 48-hour removal anyway once any sharing exists. Design out misuse: own face only, single face, live selfie default, reference photos hair-cropped on device, adults only, no renders of others.

**Stores, even on the web.** Consent is shaped to Apple's DPLA 3.3.3(K) Face Data clause and App Review Guideline 5.1.2(i) (explicit permission before sharing data "with third-party AI"; in force since the 2025-11-13 revision, page last updated 2026-10-16) and to Google Play's rule that the disclosure immediately precedes consent, so a native shell would need no redesign. The Android Developers Blog post of 2026-08-25 on AI image apps is real and is guidance, not new policy: reviewer test accounts must reach every AI feature, and you should keep documentation of the safety prompts you tested.

**Vendor posture.** Vertex AI: contractual no-training, a 24-hour cache that can be disabled per project, an abuse-logging exception path; the log retention window (30 versus 90 days in different sources) is unresolved and must be read on the live data-governance page before the consent copy is written. Anthropic: no training, ephemeral images, not retained by default except Covered Models (30 days), flagged content up to 2 years, ZDR per organisation via sales and without CORS. OpenAI: no training, 30-day abuse logs, image endpoints ZDR-eligible for gpt-image models only by sales approval, CSAM-flagged images retained even under ZDR. fal: 30-day payload retention unless `X-Fal-Store-IO: 0`, and uploaded input files are not covered by that header, so pass images inline; partner models receive client content. Replicate: one-hour auto-delete (secondary), no DPA found. BFL direct: training policy contested and unverified; avoid until read.

## 10. Test data and evaluation

Tier 0: consented friend photos, 10 to 15 per person across lighting, pose, glasses, hat and hair up or down, gitignored, with a consent manifest that names every ring A vendor and the revocation terms; the set must include at least two 3C to 4C subjects with dark skin, grey hair on a light background, a bald subject, men's medium and long hair, braids or locs, and frame-truncated long hair. Tier 1: about 50 SFHQ synthetic subjects (MIT) as the shippable CI golden set and the only faces sent to unvetted vendors; FairFace (CC BY 4.0) for the bias and abstention probe. Tier 2: research sets (CelebAMask-HQ, FFHQ, Figaro-1k, K-Hairstyle references) and InsightFace strictly offline; every one of them is non-commercial or unlicensed. The golden set is frozen with hashes; every render is keyed by model, prompt version, image id and seed. Offline metrics: ArcFace cosine with floors calibrated on real same-person pairs (DeepFace's generic thresholds are not enough, and a hairstyle change itself lowers similarity); masked LPIPS or DreamSim outside the dilated mask union; hair-mask IoU band and Lab colour delta (verify the parser's hair index, which is 17, 13, 14 or 10 depending on the checkpoint); the VIEScore-style VLM rubric; no FID at this scale. CLIP is a weak hairstyle matcher (HairPairs: ROC-AUC 0.80 on ten positive pairs), so use it only for coarse class checks. Textured-hair and grey-hair subsets get their own thresholds, and a "this did not work for my hair" feedback tag blocks a provider from becoming the default. Blind friend A/B on every model or prompt change: five raters, about 20 originals, "still me", "the style I asked for" and "would show a stylist" on 1 to 5 plus failure tags, bootstrap confidence intervals, Bradley-Terry win rates. Analysis consistency: two separate calls per photo (two prompt variants, since temperature is unavailable on the 5.x models) and two crops, scored on enums and taxonomy ids. Safety-prompt tests documented in the repo.

The cheap experiments that close the honesty gaps in section 0, about $5 to $10 in total: 20 synthetic faces through Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Gemini on Vertex for enum agreement, refusals, latency and thinking tokens; 10 to 30 Nano Banana 2.1 renders for latency and refusals; 10 two-image reference prompts; 5 to 10 dark-skin textured-hair images through the MediaPipe prototype.

## 14. What would change the plan

- An independent controlled hair-only identity benchmark appears, or the phase 2 bake-off on friends' photos shows another vetted editor clearly ahead of Nano Banana 2.1 on identity pass rate or the textured-hair subset: swap the primary through the provider interface after a harness re-run and update the consent copy.
- Vertex declines the abuse-monitoring exception for a hobby project, 2.1 is unavailable in any acceptable region, or the primary docs state a 90-day abuse-log window with no way out: fall back to the global endpoint, and if the retention story is still unacceptable, promote self-hosted FLUX 3 Image Klein or fal-hosted FLUX 3 (once fal's partner-model flag and DPA are read) earlier than phase 3.
- Measured backgrounded-tab render loss exceeds a few percent, or a provider's latency exceeds the function limit: introduce one durable runner (Trigger.dev) with private-bucket keys and delete-on-completion, and name that processor in the consent notice.
- Vercel Hobby's terms or its body and duration limits bite in phase 0: move to Cloudflare Workers; nothing in the architecture changes.
- Nano Banana latency p95 measured in phase 0 is above about 30 s: cut candidates per look from two to one plus a bald-first chain only when needed, lean harder on the progressive reveal, and reconsider a faster tier.
- A vendor ships live photoreal hairstyle replacement that holds identity on video: revisit the native shell and tier 4, reusing the client-agnostic API.
- You decide to monetise, open the app beyond friends, or charge anything: licensing walls (InsightFace, StyleGAN2 FFHQ weights, research datasets, the FLUX Kommunity revenue threshold), Vercel Pro, the TAKE IT DOWN covered-platform analysis, formal DPAs and SCCs, and BIPA exposure at scale all move from notes to blockers.
- The Anthropic usage-policy revision effective 2026-11-12 removes the consent carve-out for analysis, or Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5 become Covered Models with mandatory retention: move analysis to Gemini on Vertex (paid, cache disabled) and re-run the consistency probe.
- OpenAI grants self-serve ZDR or modified abuse monitoring for image edits, or Google publishes Developer API ZDR eligibility for small projects: the fallback could become a default-eligible renderer and the Developer API could be reconsidered.
- H-Adapter or DPF release code under a permissive licence, or HairFastGAN's StyleGAN2 dependency is replaced: revisit the reference-hair specialist and the "inspired by" framing.
- The MediaPipe GPU delegate measures under about 8 fps for the hair mask on friends' phones, or the iOS 27 home-screen camera path still rotates frames: cut the live recolor preview and keep the viewfinder behind its flag.
- Friends include anyone under 18, or several EEA or UK friends join: under-18s stay excluded entirely (Gemini API terms, no age estimation); EEA or UK friends require the paid tier, an Article 13 notice and SCCs before their first photo.
- The phase 2 blind A/B fails the textured-hair subset on every vetted vendor: ship with an explicit in-app limitation for those curl patterns, keep the bald-first chain and the stylist-reviewed bank, and prioritise a specialist experiment in phase 3 over reference transfer.

## 16. How the evidence was gathered, and its limits

A 48-agent sweep ran nine research lanes and three prototypes in parallel, then independent fact-checkers re-checked every decision-critical claim and the first twelve numbers per lane against primary sources (about 8.7M tokens, 3,100 tool calls, 4.3 hours). A second pass with a fresh search budget filled the most important gaps (product reviews and prices, editing prices and policies, hair-edit evidence, platform facts, privacy law), then three planners wrote competing plans, three judges scored them, one synthesis merged them and a critic checked the result against the original brief. This document is that synthesis after hand editing against the critic's list; the agent drafts are in `planning/`.

Limits: the web-search budget ran out partway through the first sweep, so several lanes could only use GitHub-hosted sources, official docs repositories, model cards and mirrors; the sandbox proxy blocked most vendor sites, app stores, Reddit, arXiv and Hugging Face throughout, so many figures are mirror- or snippet-sourced and are labelled so; no image-generation API could be called; app-store review themes are inferred from aggregators. The field moves monthly: OpenAI retired gpt-image-1 three days after this was written, Google released Nano Banana 2.1 two weeks before, and the Anthropic usage policy changes on 2026-11-12. Re-verify before you build.
