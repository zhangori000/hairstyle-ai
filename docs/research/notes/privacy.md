## 0. Bottom line (decision-relevant gist)

1. **A hairstyle try-on is not "facial recognition", but it can still be "biometric processing" under Illinois BIPA**, because BIPA's definition of *biometric identifier* ("scan of hand or face geometry") does not require an identification purpose, and plaintiffs have sued virtual try-on tools (eyewear, cosmetics) on exactly this theory. Eyewear cases were dismissed under BIPA's health-care exemption; a hair try-on has no such exemption, and a cosmetics case (MAC) survived a motion to dismiss in June 2026 [SECONDARY]. Cost of compliance is low (written/e-signed release, published retention schedule, no sale, vendor disclosure consent), so do it even for a friends-only app.
2. **Under GDPR/UK GDPR, a face photo is personal data but not Article 9 "biometric data" unless processed by specific technical means for the purpose of uniquely identifying someone** (Recital 51, EDPB 3/2019 §5, ICO guidance). On-device landmark detection for alignment is "specific technical processing" but not for unique identification, so it is not special-category data, but a DPIA is still prudent (innovative tech + images of faces) and consent is the cleanest basis for sending photos to third-party AI.
3. **Provider choice is the single biggest privacy lever.** Verified or mirrored defaults: Anthropic API: no training, DPA, 30-day default retention, ZDR for approved orgs. Gemini API *unpaid* tier: used to improve products and human-reviewed, 18+ only, do not submit personal data [MIRROR]; paid tier: no training, abuse logs ~55 days, ZDR by request. Vertex AI: contractual no-training, 24 h cache (disable for ZDR), region pinning. OpenAI: no training, 30-day abuse logs, ZDR via sales, image inputs CSAM-scanned and kept if flagged even under ZDR. Replicate: API predictions deleted after 1 hour. fal.ai: request JSON kept 30 days unless `X-Fal-Store-IO: 0`, CDN outputs public-by-URL unless ACL. **Black Forest Labs direct API trains on inputs by default (email opt-out)** and Stability may train by default (self-serve opt-out). Seedream via BytePlus: region-isolated endpoints (Johor, Dublin) but contractual terms unverified.
4. **Both app stores now treat "sending photos to third-party AI" as a disclosure-and-consent event.** Apple 5.1.2(i) (Oct 16, 2026 text) requires explicit permission before sharing personal data "with third-party AI"; the Apple DPLA "Face Data" clause forbids off-device transfer without clear and conspicuous consent; Google Play's User Data policy explicitly applies to third-party AI integrations, requires prominent in-app disclosure before runtime permission, requires the Photo Picker for one-off selection, and its AI-Generated Content policy requires in-app report/flag plus prevention of deepfake/NCII outputs. An Android Developers Blog post of Aug 25, 2026 reportedly added moderation and test-account requirements for apps that generate or edit images of people [SECONDARY].
5. **Deepfake and NCII law is live.** TAKE IT DOWN Act (Pub. L. 119-12, signed May 19, 2025) criminalises publishing "digital forgeries" and requires covered platforms to run a 48-hour notice-and-removal process by May 19, 2026 [MIRROR]. EU AI Act Art. 50 applies from Aug 2, 2026: providers must machine-readably mark synthetic images; deployers must disclose deep fakes; purely personal, non-professional use is outside the deployer duty (Commission guidelines example) [MIRROR]. A hairstyle edit of your own face is low risk; editing a *friend's* face without the friend's consent is the risk vector to design out.
6. **Recommended posture: "Tier 1 ephemeral cloud" by default, with "Tier 0 device-only" for detection/cropping and an opt-in "Tier 2 encrypted library".** Photos never touch your own storage; EXIF stripped and face-cropped on device; sent straight to a no-training provider with the shortest retention you can get; results live only on the device unless the user opts in to a library with per-user deletion and auto-expiry.

## 1. Is a hairstyle try-on "biometric processing"? Jurisdiction by jurisdiction

### 1.1 Illinois BIPA (740 ILCS 14) — [MIRROR of ilga.gov text, incl. P.A. 103-769]

Mirror: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CloudSecurityAlliance-DataSets/dataset-public-laws-regulations-standards/main/regulation/illinois.gov/bipa/bipa.md (official: https://www.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/ilcs3.asp?ActID=3004&ChapterID=57)

- Definition: "'Biometric identifier' means a retina or iris scan, fingerprint, voiceprint, or scan of hand or face geometry. Biometric identifiers do not include writing samples, written signatures, **photographs**, ... or physical descriptions such as height, weight, hair color, or eye color." Also excluded: "information captured from a patient in a health care setting" (the hook used by eyewear try-on defendants).
- "'Biometric information' means any information, regardless of how it is captured, converted, stored, or shared, based on an individual's biometric identifier **used to identify an individual**."
- "'Written release' means informed written consent, **electronic signature**, or, in the context of employment, a release executed by an employee..." (Source: P.A. 103-769, eff. 8-2-24).
- §15(a): written, public policy with a retention schedule; destroy when the purpose is satisfied or within 3 years of the last interaction, whichever first.
- §15(b): before collection, inform in writing that a biometric identifier is being collected/stored, the specific purpose and length of term, and receive a written release.
- §15(c): no selling, leasing, trading or otherwise profiting. §15(d): no disclosure/redisclosure unless the subject consents (or a financial transaction, law, warrant). Note: sending a face scan to a cloud vendor is a "disclosure" — get consent that names the vendor class.
- §15(e): reasonable standard of care; at least as protective as other confidential data.
- §20: $1,000 per negligent violation / $5,000 per intentional or reckless violation (or actual damages), fees, injunction. **§20(b)-(c) (2024 amendment):** repeated collection of the same identifier from the same person by the same method is a *single violation* with at most one recovery; same for repeated disclosure to the same recipient.
- Seventh Circuit, *Gregg v. Central Transport* (Apr. 1, 2026): the amendment applies retroactively to pending cases [MIRROR: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-agreements/open-agreements/main/practice-guides/privacy/us/illinois.md ; corroborated by https://raw.githubusercontent.com/smerphy/cyber-grc-agent-skills/068b0616e83695a0ab236353d253780987738261/context/regulations/us-biometric-privacy-laws.md]. *Cothron v. White Castle* (Ill. Feb. 17, 2023) had held per-scan accrual; *Tims* gives a five-year limitations period.
- **Photos vs face geometry:** "Plaintiffs have litigated faceprints computed *from* photographs under the *scan of face geometry* language, so photo-based systems still deserve BIPA review" [MIRROR open-agreements]. "Photographs and information derived from them are excluded from the definition, but face-geometry scans derived from photographs are not — that is the fault line in the case law" [MIRROR: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ThomasMoreAI/legal-skills-open/2896ce4c0ca1632b43efb29647c2811eadc89247/us/data-protection/skills/legal-us-clemensjl/references/health-and-biometrics.md].
- **Virtual try-on cases** [SECONDARY, research note dated 2026, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shakaibarsalan/tryon/cbbe110a90b80b76c7ac9620a92c8664e5a5f989/docs/research/alternatives-research.md]: "eyewear cases were dismissed under the health-care exemption, but the MAC Cosmetics motion to dismiss was denied in June 2026 and the case continues. Apparel has no healthcare exemption." [KNOWLEDGE, verify: *Warmack-Stillwell v. Christian Dior Perfumes* (N.D. Ill. Feb. 2023) and *Svoboda v. Frames for America* (N.D. Ill. 2022) dismissed eyewear try-on claims because non-prescription sunglasses are FDA Class I medical devices; *Theriot v. Louis Vuitton N. Am.* (S.D.N.Y. 2023) and *Kukovec v. Estée Lauder* (N.D. Ill. 2022) let try-on BIPA claims proceed past dismissal.] A hair try-on cannot use the health-care exemption.
- **Does on-device landmark extraction count?** BIPA has no on-device or no-transmission exemption; the identifier is the "scan of face geometry" itself. Defence arguments (no identification, no storage, user-controlled device) have had mixed success and are fact-bound. Practical rule: assume a MediaPipe/Vision landmark pass is a "scan", obtain the §15(b) release *before* the camera opens, publish the retention schedule ("landmarks exist only in memory for the duration of the edit; never written to disk or transmitted"), and never persist templates.
- Clearview BIPA class settlement approved Mar. 20, 2025 (N.D. Ill.) [MIRROR smerphy]. Illinois has no regulator path; enforcement is private litigation only (*Rosenbach*) [MIRROR open-agreements].

### 1.2 Texas CUBI (Bus. & Com. Code §503.001) — [MIRROR summaries; official text https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/BC/htm/BC.503.htm not reachable]

- Notice and consent before capturing a biometric identifier (retina/iris, fingerprint, voiceprint, hand/face geometry) for a commercial purpose; no sale/lease/disclosure without consent (listed exceptions); reasonable care; destroy "within a reasonable time, but not later than the first anniversary of the date the purpose for collecting the identifier expires"; civil penalty up to $25,000 per violation; Attorney General only, no private right of action [MIRROR ThomasMoreAI; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mukul975/Privacy-Data-Protection-Skills/main/skills/privacy/texas-tdpsa-compliance/references/standards.md].
- Enforcement is real: Texas v. Meta settled for $1.4 billion (July 30, 2024); Google settled for $1.375 billion (May 9, 2025) [MIRROR smerphy, with AG press-release URLs].
- Texas HB 149 (signed June 22, 2025, effective Jan 1, 2026) amended CUBI with AI-training and security carve-outs [MIRROR smerphy] — verify the exact text before relying on it.

### 1.3 Washington — RCW 19.375 (biometric identifiers) and the My Health My Data Act (ch. 19.373 RCW)

- RCW 19.375 (2017): notice/consent for enrolling biometric identifiers in a database for a commercial purpose; AG enforcement under the CPA; no private right of action; no reported enforcement located as of Sept 2026 [MIRROR smerphy].
- MHMDA [MIRROR: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/open-agreements/open-agreements/618e0a3ef5fd79fe13603787fb6c0c21077343be/practice-guides/privacy/us/washington.md, last updated June 11, 2026]: a "regulated entity" is any legal entity that conducts business in Washington or targets Washington consumers and determines the purposes/means of collecting "consumer health data" (RCW 19.373.010(23)); "consumer health data" is personal information linked or reasonably linkable to a consumer that identifies physical or mental health status, and the statutory list expressly includes **biometric data**; a violation is a per se Consumer Protection Act violation with a private right of action; **no revenue floor**; small businesses (<100,000 consumers/yr, or <25,000 and <50% revenue) are covered, not exempt (later compliance dates in 2024).
- Biometric data under MHMDA (RCW 19.373.010(4)) [KNOWLEDGE, verify at https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.373.010]: data generated from measurement or technological processing of physiological/biological/behavioural characteristics that identifies a consumer, including "imagery of the iris, retina, fingerprint, **face**, hand, palm, vein patterns, and voice recordings, from which an identifier template can be extracted". A face photo "from which a template can be extracted" is therefore arguably consumer health data if linked to the person. MHMDA permits collection "to the extent necessary to provide a product or service that the consumer ... has requested" without separate consent, but *sharing* requires separate consent and *selling* requires a signed authorization. Design implication: the try-on itself is "necessary to provide the requested service"; sending photos to a vendor as a processor under contract is processing, not sharing; never share with anyone else without a distinct opt-in.

### 1.4 California CCPA/CPRA — [KNOWLEDGE; official definitions at https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.140]

- "Biometric information" (§1798.140(c)) includes "imagery of the iris, retina, fingerprint, face, hand, palm, vein patterns, and voice recordings, from which an identifier template, such as a faceprint ... can be extracted". "Sensitive personal information" (§1798.140(ae)) includes "the processing of biometric information for the purpose of uniquely identifying a consumer" — so a try-on that does not identify is *personal information* but not SPI; disclose retention period in the notice at collection.
- Thresholds (§1798.140(d)): >$26.625M annual revenue (CPPA adjustment effective Jan 1, 2025), or buy/sell/share PI of 100,000+ consumers/households, or 50%+ revenue from selling/sharing [MIRROR open-agreements California guide]. A friends-only app will not be a "business"; a public app might. Also CPPA regulations: risk assessments for processing SPI; cybersecurity audits from 2028 [MIRROR].
- California deepfake NCII civil claim: Civ. Code §1708.86 [MIRROR vorplabs dataset].

### 1.5 Colorado — HB24-1130 biometric amendments to the CPA (C.R.S. §6-1-1314), effective July 1, 2025 [MIRROR smerphy; open-agreements Colorado guide]

- The CPA applies to a controller "controlling or processing **any amount** of biometric identifiers or biometric data" regardless of the 100,000/25,000-consumer thresholds; a controller that qualifies only through the biometric trigger must comply only for that data [MIRROR open-agreements].
- Requires a written policy with a retention schedule and deletion at the earliest of purpose satisfied / 24 months after last interaction / 45 days after an annual review finds storage unnecessary; consent before collection; AG and DAs enforce; no private right of action; no biometric rulemaking under §6-1-1314(7) located as of Sept 2026 [MIRROR smerphy]. Official bill page: https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1130 ; signed act PDF: https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/2024a_1130_signed.pdf.
- [KNOWLEDGE, verify] Colorado's definition excludes a photograph or video unless it is used to produce a biometric identifier — the on-device landmark pass is the thing to analyse.

### 1.6 Other US states and federal overlay

- Every comprehensive state privacy law (CT, OR, TX TDPSA, VA, etc.) treats "biometric data ... processed for the purpose of uniquely identifying" as sensitive data requiring opt-in consent and an assessment [MIRROR ThomasMoreAI]. Oregon's definition [KNOWLEDGE] excludes facial mapping/geometry unless used to identify; Texas TDPSA: no revenue threshold, AG only, $7,500 per violation [MIRROR open-agreements Texas].
- New York S.1422A (Biometric Identifier Privacy Act) passed the Senate June 3, 2026 and was delivered to the Assembly [MIRROR smerphy]; NYC's biometric law applies to commercial establishments (not apps).
- FTC: Policy Statement on Biometric Information and §5 (May 18, 2023); *FTC v. Rite Aid* order (Mar 8, 2024) [MIRROR smerphy]. [KNOWLEDGE] *Everalbum* (2021) ordered deletion of models trained on users' photos ("algorithmic disgorgement") — do not fine-tune on users' faces without explicit consent.
- COPPA [KNOWLEDGE]: photos/videos containing a child's image are "personal information"; the 2025 COPPA rule amendments (effective June 23, 2025; compliance by April 22, 2026) add biometric identifiers. Combined with the Gemini API ban on under-18 audiences (see §2), gate cloud features to adults.

### 1.7 EU GDPR / EU AI Act

- GDPR Art. 4(14): biometric data = personal data resulting from **specific technical processing** relating to physical/physiological/behavioural characteristics "which allow or confirm the unique identification" of a person, such as facial images. Art. 9(1) prohibits processing "biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person" absent an Art. 9(2) condition [MIRROR: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/devrim-1283/kvkk-wiki/a8f0a64caaf421c864eafa707eee9675251fe645/gdpr/10-special-topics/biometric-data.md ; GDPR text https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj].
- Recital 51: photographs are not systematically biometric data; they are only covered "when processed through a specific technical means allowing the unique identification or authentication of a natural person" [KNOWLEDGE of the recital text; https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-51/]. The kvkk-wiki summary: "A photograph used illustratively is not Article 9 data; the same photograph fed into a face-matching algorithm to identify the person becomes Article 9 data when the algorithm operates."
- EDPB Guidelines 3/2019 on video devices, §5 [KNOWLEDGE, https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-32019-processing-personal-data-through-video_en]: three cumulative criteria (nature, means/technical processing, purpose of uniquely identifying); the example of a system that detects characteristics (e.g., for targeted advertising) without uniquely identifying is not Art. 9 processing but still personal data requiring a lawful basis and, usually, a DPIA. EDPB 5/2022 (facial recognition in law enforcement) gives the necessity/proportionality framework [MIRROR kvkk].
- ICO (UK): "Biometric recognition describes when you use biometric data to uniquely identify someone"; "If you are using a biometric recognition system, you are processing special category biometric data" [MIRROR quoting ICO: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bearnl/toolbox/26e7888f670ddd14803be416f1095a8902d5d102/paper2_lit_audit/04_privacy_thesis_AUDIT_COMPLETE.md ; ICO guidance https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/biometric-data-guidance-biometric-recognition/]. A try-on is not biometric recognition.
- Consequence: lawful basis for the try-on itself can be consent (cleanest, and what the app stores require anyway); for a friends-only hobby app the GDPR household exemption (Art. 2(2)(c)) may apply to the *users*, but the *developer* operating the service is a controller once it runs a backend. Do a lightweight DPIA (Art. 35; biometric/innovative-technology criteria) and sign DPAs with vendors; international transfers need SCCs (Anthropic DPA includes SCC Modules 2/3 and the UK addendum [LIVE]).
- EU AI Act (Reg. (EU) 2024/1689) [MIRROR of authoritative text: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mcekikj/eu-ai-act-knowledge-graph/main/EU-AI-Act-Vault/01%20Articles/Article%2050.md ; eur-lex https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=OJ:L_202401689#art_50]:
  - Art. 50(2): providers of systems generating synthetic image/audio/video/text "shall ensure that the outputs ... are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated", unless the system performs "an assistive function for standard editing or do[es] not substantially alter the input data".
  - Art. 50(4): deployers of a system that generates or manipulates image content "constituting a deep fake, shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated" (lighter duty for evidently artistic/creative works).
  - Art. 50(5): information "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure".
  - Art. 113: the Regulation applies from **2 August 2026** [MIRROR Article 113].
  - Commission Guidelines on Art. 50 (C(2026) 5054, annex) [MIRROR of the text: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Klotzkette/claude-fuer-deutsches-recht/main/quality/ki-verordnung-2026-10-09/quellen/transparenz-leitlinien-2026.txt]: transparency obligations apply "as from 2 August 2026"; example: "the provider of the AI system used to generate deep fakes to feature on a personal Christmas card should mark those deep fakes in a machine-readable manner ... At the same time, the use of the system by natural persons for purely personal, non-professional activities will not be covered by the deployer obligation laid down [in] Article 50(4)"; recommends persistent icons/watermarks/"AI" symbols and multi-modal cues; references the "Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content" published 10 June 2026.
  - Penalty: Art. 99(4)(g) up to EUR 15,000,000 or 3% of worldwide turnover (lower of the two for SMEs under Art. 99(6)) [SECONDARY: OpenNutriTracker legal notes, 2026-08-02, https://github.com/simonoppowa/OpenNutriTracker/blob/main/docs/ai-legal-constraints.md (local copy: mirrors/opennutri-ai-legal-constraints.md)]. The same note reports the "Digital Omnibus" (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) did **not** defer Article 50 (it changed 50(7) and added a transitional Art. 111(4)) [SECONDARY, verify on EUR-Lex].
  - Definitions (Art. 3) [MIRROR]: biometric data (33), biometric identification (35), biometric verification (36), emotion recognition (39), biometric categorisation (40: "assigning natural persons to specific categories on the basis of their biometric data, unless it is ancillary to another commercial service and strictly necessary for objective technical reasons"). Inferring face shape/skin tone to pick hairstyles is categorisation on biometric data but plausibly "ancillary ... and strictly necessary" for the service; avoid inferring protected attributes (ethnicity etc.) — Anthropic's AUP also forbids biometric categorisation to infer race, religion, sexual orientation etc. [LIVE].
  - Who is "provider" vs "deployer": if you build the app on Gemini/OpenAI/Flux, the model vendor is the provider of the generating system (and SynthID/C2PA marking is their 50(2) duty), you are a deployer (50(4)) toward your users; if your users use it for personal purposes they are outside 50(4). Keep the provider's marks intact (do not strip C2PA/SynthID) and show an "AI-edited" label on results and exports.

### 1.8 UK, Canada

- UK GDPR mirrors EU analysis; ICO biometric guidance above; the UK Online Safety Act duties fall on services with UGC/sharing, not on a private try-on [SECONDARY devsemih summary].
- Canada: PIPEDA; the OPC's Clearview AI finding (PIPEDA-2021-001, Feb 2021) held that mass scraping of facial images for a recognition service was collection of sensitive biometric data requiring **express consent**, rejected the "publicly available" argument, and recommended ceasing service and deleting Canadians' data [MIRROR summary: https://github.com/Sibelius-6/mdf/blob/main/content/1259/soc305.md ; official: https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/investigations/investigations-into-businesses/2021/pipeda-2021-001/]. [KNOWLEDGE] Quebec Law 25 / the Act to establish a legal framework for IT (s. 44-45) requires disclosing biometric *identity verification* systems to the CAI before use; not triggered by a try-on, but Quebec treats biometrics as sensitive.

### 1.9 Minors

- Gemini API terms [MIRROR]: "You must be 18 years of age or older to use the APIs. You also will not use the Services as part of a website, application, or other service ... that is directed towards or is likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18."
- Apple 5.1.4 and Google Play Families/Child Safety policies [LIVE]; Anthropic AUP: products serving minors must follow Anthropic's minors guidelines [LIVE].
- Design: 18+ self-declaration gate at least for cloud features; no age *estimation* from the face (that is biometric categorisation and a prominent regulatory target).

## 3. App store rules

### 3.1 Apple App Store Review Guidelines [LIVE, "Updated: October 16, 2026", https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/]

- 5.1.1(i): privacy policy must "Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion". (ii) consent for data collection "even if such data is considered to be anonymous"; easy withdrawal. (iii) data minimisation: "use the out-of-process picker or a share sheet rather than requesting full access to protected resources like Photos". (v) in-app account deletion if accounts exist; let people use the app without login where possible. (viii) no compiling personal information from sources other than the user.
- **5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so."** (ii) no repurposing without further consent. (vi) data from "depth and/or facial mapping tools (such as ARKit, Camera APIs, or Photo APIs) may not be used for marketing, advertising or use-based data mining, including by third parties."
- 1.2 User-Generated Content now expressly covers "AI content creation apps" and requires: filtering objectionable material, a reporting mechanism with timely responses, blocking abusive users, published contact info; apps used for "objectification of real people" or NCII "do not belong on the App Store".
- 2.5.13 (face recognition for auth must use LocalAuthentication); 2.5.14 explicit consent and visible indicator when recording camera input; 5.1.4 kids/COPPA.
- **Apple Developer Program License Agreement, §3.3 "K. Face Data"** [LIVE, https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/]: use Face Data "only to provide a service or function that is directly relevant to the use of the Application"; "inform users of Your intended uses and disclosures of Face Data ... and obtain clear and conspicuous consent ... before any collection or use"; no advertising/marketing/profiling; no transfer to analytics providers/data brokers; "Face Data may not be shared or transferred off the user's device unless You have obtained clear and conspicuous consent for the transfer and the Face Data is used only in fulfilling a specific service or function for Your Application (e.g., a face mesh is used to display an image of the user within the Application)"; require service providers to honour the same limits.
- App privacy details ("nutrition labels") [LIVE, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/]: "Sensitive Info" includes "biometric data"; "Photos or Videos" is its own type; **"Collect" refers to transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time** — so a truly ephemeral try-on (no server storage, provider retention only for the request) can honestly declare photos as not collected, but a 30-day abuse log at the provider is arguably "longer than necessary to service the request"; declare Photos as collected, "App Functionality", not linked, not used for tracking, unless you can get ZDR. ## 4. Safety: non-consensual editing, deepfakes, minors, NSFW, celebrity references

### 4.1 TAKE IT DOWN Act — Public Law 119-12, May 19, 2025 [MIRROR of enrolled text: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Squidito/legislationpatch/main/data/bill-text/119-S-146.txt ; https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/146]

- §2 criminal prohibition: knowingly publishing an intimate visual depiction or a "digital forgery" of an identifiable individual without consent where it is intended to cause or causes harm; stricter rules for minors.
- "Digital forgery" = "any intimate visual depiction of an identifiable individual created through the use of software, machine learning, artificial intelligence, or any other computer-generated or technological means, including by adapting, modifying, manipulating, or altering an authentic visual depiction, that, when viewed as a whole by a reasonable person, is indistinguishable from an authentic visual depiction of the individual."
- §3: "Not later than 1 year after the date of enactment" (i.e., by May 19, 2026) a covered platform must establish a notice-and-removal process and "not later than 48 hours after receiving such request" remove the depiction and known copies; good-faith removal safe harbour; FTC enforces as an unfair or deceptive practice.
- Relevance: a hairstyle app that never publishes images to other users is probably not a "covered platform" [KNOWLEDGE: definition targets services that primarily host user-generated content or publish NCII in the ordinary course]; but if you add sharing, implement a reporting and 48-hour removal path anyway and keep hair edits strictly non-intimate (block NSFW inputs/outputs).

### 4.2 State deepfake and likeness laws [MIRROR of a dated tracker dataset, 2026-07-30: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vorplabs/research-datasets/81f2b17aefa638d6457876df549695f54d2b6f07/datasets/deepfake-laws/2026.07.30/deepfake-laws.csv]

- California Civ. Code §1708.86 (civil liability for sexually explicit deepfakes); Tennessee ELVIS Act (effective July 1, 2024; right of publicity covers AI replicas of voice and likeness); California AB 1836/AB 2602 (2024; digital replicas of performers); Pennsylvania SB 649 / 18 Pa.C.S. §4101.1 (effective Sept 5, 2025: general "forged digital likeness" offence with an affirmative defence where viewers were put on notice the content is not genuine — a reason to label edited images); Delaware 10 Del. C. ch. 78 and Montana 45-5-640 (NCII incl. AI).
- Federal: DEFIANCE Act (S.1837) passed the Senate Jan 13, 2026, not enacted as of July 2026; NO FAKES Act reintroduced in the 119th Congress, not enacted as of July 2026 [MIRROR dataset].

### 4.3 Friend uploads someone else's face (non-consensual editing)

- Legal exposure is mostly the subject's: privacy torts, state deepfake statutes if intimate/deceptive, GDPR (the subject is a data subject whose data you process without a basis). Platform rules (Apple 1.2, Play AI-Generated Content) make *you* responsible for safeguards.
- Design controls: default to live selfie capture (front camera) with the consent screen; for gallery uploads require an attestation ("This is me, or I have this person's permission"); single-face only (reject multi-face images); block known-minor signals via self-declared age, not face-based age estimation; no bulk upload; no share-to-others without the subject's own account; keep results on the uploader's device; add report/flag UI even for a friends-only app (Play requires it).
- Moderation: run an NSFW classifier on input and output (on-device option: a small NSFW model via TF.js/ONNX; provider side: Gemini/OpenAI safety filters are non-configurable for image outputs [SECONDARY]). Refuse hair edits that also change body/clothing; constrain prompts to hair attributes (a typed prompt builder, not free text, is the cheapest safety control).
- Record the Anthropic AUP constraints when using Claude for analysis: no facial recognition/identification, no biometric categorisation inferring protected attributes, no NCII [LIVE].

### 4.4 "Hair reference photo of a celebrity" (private use)

- Copyright: the photo's photographer/agency owns copyright; a private, non-commercial, transformative use (extracting a hairstyle to render on your own face, never redistributing the reference) sits at the low-risk end of fair use; do not store or re-serve the reference image, do not build a catalogue of celebrity photos, and do not use celebrity names/images in marketing or the style catalogue.
- Right of publicity/likeness: statutes (CA Civ. Code §3344, NY Civ. Rights Law §§50-51, TN ELVIS Act) target commercial use/advertising; a user's private try-on using a celebrity's hair, not face, does not reproduce the celebrity's likeness in the output. [KNOWLEDGE]
- EU: the person in a reference photo is a data subject; process the reference ephemerally, crop to the hair region on device before any upload, never persist, and do not send the celebrity's full face to a vendor if you can avoid it (hair-only crop plus a text description works for most models).
- Trademark/branding: do not name styles after celebrities in the catalogue ("the Rachel" is folklore, but "Taylor Swift bob" invites a complaint).

### 4.5 Watermarking and provenance of outputs

- Gemini API image outputs carry an invisible SynthID watermark on all image models (measured 2026-09-24) and Google C2PA manifests; the visible "sparkle" is tier-gated and absent on API output; a consumer "Media watermark" setting appeared Aug 14, 2026 [SECONDARY, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiltodelta/remove-ai-watermarks/540d5648cc271110439be8aeaf6626cc4d909b4e/docs/synthid.md and docs/watermarking-landscape.md]. OpenAI gpt-image outputs carry C2PA manifests; FLUX outputs carry a C2PA manifest (issuer "Black Forest Labs") plus the open `imwatermark` DWT-DCT mark (fragile); fal signs outputs with C2PA + invisible watermark; Stability signs C2PA; BytePlus signs C2PA as "Byteplus Pte. Ltd." [SECONDARY].
- Practical: keep provider C2PA manifests intact when exporting (re-encoding via canvas strips them — if you re-encode, re-sign with `c2pa` / `@contentauth/c2pa-web` and add an `c2pa.actions` "edited with AI" assertion); add a visible "AI-edited preview" label on exports to stylists; C2PA 2.4 "Durable Content Credentials" (April 2026) add soft bindings for metadata-stripped files [SECONDARY]. NIST AI 600-1 treats watermarking, metadata, fingerprints and human review as complementary [SECONDARY MoodX note].

## 5. Minimisation design and the recommended posture

### 5.1 Tiers

**Tier 0 — Device-only (free, strongest privacy, weakest results).** Face detection, landmarks, hair segmentation, EXIF stripping, cropping and simple overlays all on device (MediaPipe Face Landmarker 468 points / Apple Vision + ARKit face mesh / TF.js face-landmarks-detection / `@vladmandic/human`). No network for images. Privacy label: Data Not Collected. Use this tier for everything *before* a user consents to a cloud edit (preview, framing, lighting checks) and for the "hair only" crop of reference photos. BIPA note: the landmark pass is still a "scan of face geometry" on the plaintiff theory; cover it with the same consent screen and state that landmarks are never persisted or transmitted.

**Tier 1 — Ephemeral cloud (recommended default).** The device prepares a normalised JPEG (longest side 1024 px, EXIF removed by re-encoding, face-cropped with margin), shows the disclosure, and sends it directly to the model vendor through a thin relay that holds the API key, enforces rate limits and never writes image bytes to disk or logs (or uses a client-side key-exchange pattern so the relay never sees bytes). Provider selection by posture: Anthropic (analysis/suggestions), Vertex AI with caching disabled and region pinned or OpenAI with ZDR/residency if granted (rendering), Replicate for 1-hour-deletion hosted open models, fal only with `X-Fal-Store-IO: 0`, `sync_mode`/data URIs and private ACLs. Never the Gemini unpaid tier, BFL direct, or Stability without opt-out. Results return to the device and are stored only there (HairMirror's pattern: on-device history of up to 20 results, SHA-256 digest of the prepared JPEG used as a local cache key and never uploaded [SECONDARY]). Signed URLs, if used at all, expire in minutes (BFL's delivery links expire in 10 minutes; fal CDN lifetime is configurable). Nutrition label: Photos collected for App Functionality, not linked, not tracking; disclose each vendor by name.

**Tier 2 — Stored library (opt-in only).** Only if friends want cross-device history: encrypt at rest with per-user keys (or client-side encryption so the server stores ciphertext), auto-delete after a user-chosen window (default 30 days, max 1 year to stay inside every destruction clock), per-item and per-user delete that also purges backups within 30 days, region pinning (EU bucket for EU users), no analytics on image content, vendors under DPA. Publish the retention schedule (BIPA §15(a), Colorado).

### 5.2 Consent and retention spec (outline)

1. **Pre-camera "Face data notice" screen** (BIPA §15(b) / DPLA Face Data / Apple 5.1.2(i) / Play prominent disclosure): plain-language purpose ("to render hairstyles on your photo"), what is processed (your photo; face landmarks computed on your phone; the photo is sent to [vendor] to render the style), length of term ("deleted from our relay immediately; [vendor] may keep abuse logs up to N days; results stay on your device until you delete them"), no sale, no advertising, no identification, no training; named third parties; how to withdraw (Settings > Delete my data); a checkbox or signature-style affirmative action that serves as the BIPA "written release" via electronic signature; store a consent record (text version hash, timestamp, app version) — on device, plus a minimal server record if accounts exist.
2. **Separate toggles** for: cloud rendering (default off until consented), library storage (Tier 2), uploading a photo of someone else (requires attestation), reference photos of other people (hair-only crop happens before upload).
3. **Retention schedule (public page)**: originals — not stored by us; prepared copy — memory only for the request; landmarks — memory only; results — device only (Tier 1) or encrypted library with N-day expiry (Tier 2); relay logs — request id, timestamp, status, model, token/pixel counts, no bytes, 30 days; vendor logs — per the provider table; backups — none for images.
4. **Deletion**: in-app "Delete everything" (clears device cache/history, revokes consent, deletes server records and sends deletion to vendors where an API exists, e.g. fal payload delete, Gemini Interactions delete); account deletion in-app and via web URL (Play requirement).
5. **Transparency of AI edits**: label every rendered image "AI-edited preview" in the UI and on export; keep provider C2PA; add our own C2PA action when re-encoding.
6. **Incident posture**: image bytes never in logs, crash reports or analytics (lint rule + a redaction test); API keys only on the relay; rotate keys; no third-party analytics/ads SDKs in the camera flow (also keeps the Apple label honest).

### 5.3 Engineering notes that fall out of the law

- Model the photo lifecycle as a state machine with typed, non-serialisable image handles: `Picked -> Prepared(exifStripped, cropped) -> ConsentChecked -> Sent(provider, requestId) -> Received(result) -> Discarded`. Make "Discarded" the only terminal state reachable without a user action, and make persistence an explicit, separately-consented transition.
- Events in the event log carry ids and sizes, never pixels. Add a unit test that serialises every event type and asserts no base64/image MIME content.
- Keep a `docs/privacy/providers.md` with dated quotes of each vendor's retention/training clauses (copy the format of the visualnovel/OpenNutriTracker notes) and a `docs/privacy/retention-schedule.md` that is rendered into the app's policy page.
- Prefer the OS photo picker (iOS PHPicker, Android Photo Picker) over broad photo permissions — both stores now push this.
