## B. What this means for a friends-only hairstyle app (plain language, per jurisdiction)

**Illinois (BIPA).** The MAC ruling (N.D. Ill., June 2026) means a court will let a "scan of face geometry" claim proceed against a virtual try-on even though the product never identifies anyone and starts from a photograph; the photograph exclusion does not protect geometry derived from the photo. There is no health-care hook for hair. The good news from P.A. 103-769 and *Gregg*: consent can be an electronic signature (a real tap-to-sign/checkbox record, not a buried ToS), and exposure is capped at one recovery per person per collection method and per recipient, applied retroactively. For a friends app: show a pre-camera notice (purpose, what is derived, where it goes, retention), capture an affirmative e-signature consent, publish a retention/destruction schedule, never persist landmarks/meshes, and name the cloud vendors as recipients in the consent. Illinois enforcement is private litigation only; friends are unlikely plaintiffs, but the cost of compliance is a screen and a log.

**Washington (MHMDA + RCW 19.375).** Face imagery "from which an identifier template can be extracted" counts as "biometric data", which the statute lists as "consumer health data", and the Act applies to any entity targeting Washington consumers with no revenue floor and a private right of action (treble damages capped at $25,000 per plaintiff, fees). So far, enforcement is thin: no AG actions as of mid-2026, two private class actions (Amazon ads SDK, a cannabis retailer), none about try-on apps. The Act allows collection "to the extent necessary to provide a product or service the consumer requested", so the try-on itself needs no separate consent form, but *sharing* face data with anyone other than a processor under contract needs separate consent and *selling* needs a signed authorization. Design: cloud AI vendors must be processors under written terms (DPA/terms that forbid training), friends-to-friends sharing must be an explicit per-share opt-in by the photo's subject, and a consumer-health-data privacy policy link is required. (Verbatim statute text still needs to be pulled from app.leg.wa.gov.)

**Colorado (HB24-1130 / CPA).** Biometric duties (written retention policy, consent before collection, deletion at the earliest of purpose satisfied / 24 months after last interaction / 45 days after an annual review) apply from July 1, 2025 to any controller processing any amount of biometric identifiers, with no consumer-count threshold. The carve-out that matters: photographs, recordings and "data generated from" them are not biometric data "unless used for identification" (AG rules; statute reportedly §6-1-1303(2.2)). A try-on that never identifies anyone arguably processes no Colorado "biometric data" at all; a never-persisted on-device landmark pass used only for alignment is the strongest version of that argument. Keep it that way: no face matching, no "is this the same person" features, no stored templates. AG/DA enforcement only; no private right of action.

**EU / UK (GDPR, UK GDPR).** A selfie is personal data; it becomes "biometric data" only after specific technical processing (ICO: extracting facial features by algorithm does this), and it becomes Article 9 special-category data only if that processing is for uniquely identifying someone (Recital 51; ICO). A hairstyle try-on with on-device landmarks for alignment is therefore ordinary personal-data processing, not Article 9, as long as you never run recognition/verification. Pick a lawful basis (consent is cleanest and the app stores demand it anyway), do a short DPIA (images of faces + novel tech + third-party AI), sign vendor DPAs with SCCs for US vendors, and keep the household exemption in mind: the *users* editing their own faces are private individuals, but the *developer* running a backend is a controller. Reference photos of third parties (celebrity hair) should be cropped to hair on device and never stored.

**EU AI Act (Article 50).** In force since 2 Aug 2026; the Digital Omnibus did not defer it, only gave providers of pre-existing generative systems until 2 Dec 2026 to add machine-readable marking. Your model vendor is the Art. 50(2) provider (SynthID/C2PA marks: keep them intact). Hairstyle edits are "substantial facial modification" per the guidelines' examples, so they are not within the "standard editing" exception and a photoreal edit of a real person is a deep fake. The deployer duty to label (Art. 50(4)) does not apply to "natural persons for purely personal, non-professional activities" — a friends group editing their own faces is outside it — but a stylist export is a professional-facing use and the app itself should show a visible "AI-edited" label at first exposure; it is cheap and also satisfies US state notice defences and store policies.

**US federal (TAKE IT DOWN Act).** A "covered platform" must (i) "serve the public" and (ii) primarily host user-generated content or publish NCII in the ordinary course. An invite-only app for a handful of friends with hair-only edits does not serve the public and is not primarily a UGC forum, so the 48-hour notice-and-removal duty (in force since 19 May 2026) very likely does not attach; the criminal provisions on intimate digital forgeries apply to individuals regardless. If sharing ever opens beyond invited friends, add a reporting path and 48-hour removal anyway. (No FTC interpretation of "serves the public" was found.)

**Provider terms (what to put in the consent screen).** Anthropic: no training on API content; image inputs ephemeral; conversation content not retained by default except Fable/Mythos "Covered Models" (30-day retention, no ZDR unless authorized); ZDR is sales-gated per organization and disables CORS (use a backend proxy); flagged content can be kept up to 2 years. OpenAI: 30-day abuse logs by default on the images endpoints, ZDR/MAM "subject to prior approval" via sales, CSAM-flagged images kept even under ZDR. Gemini Developer API: never the free tier (trains, human review); paid tier logs 55 days; ZDR "by request" per one 2026 note but eligibility for small projects unconfirmed; Vertex gives contractual no-training, a disable-able 24-hour cache and an abuse-logging exception path (log retention 30 vs 90 days unresolved). fal: 30-day request-history unless `X-Fal-Store-IO: 0`, public-by-URL CDN unless ACL/lifecycle set, no-training except "Pending Enterprise Ready" models, DPA excluded for those. Replicate: 1-hour auto-delete of API inputs/outputs (new accounts since Jan 2023), DPA not found. BFL direct API: training posture unresolved — avoid for faces until its privacy policy is read.

**App stores.** Apple (DPLA Aug 18, 2026; Guidelines Oct 16, 2026): an uploaded photo for facial analysis is "Face Data"; you need clear and conspicuous consent before any collection/use and a separate one before any off-device transfer, limited to the app's function, with service providers bound to the same limits, plus explicit permission before sharing with third-party AI; use PHPicker. Google Play: the User Data policy expressly covers third-party AI integrations; in-app prominent disclosure must immediately precede consent/permission; face photos are "camera data"/"Photos" (sensitive) rather than a named biometric category; use the Photo Picker to avoid READ_MEDIA_IMAGES declarations.
