# Judge panel verdicts

_Three judge agents (senior engineer, product and UX lead, privacy counsel plus finance) scored the three plans 1-10 on feasibility, product value, privacy, cost realism, technical soundness and honesty, and listed ideas to graft and flaws to fix._

# Judgements


## Lens: Senior engineer who has shipped consumer AI apps: scored on whether the architecture, typing, state model, async-job design, observability and spend controls will actually survive ten friends on iPhones, and on how an AI coding assistant will fare implementing it (single-language runtime, enforceable invariants, measurable prompt changes). All three plans share the same research base and therefore the same skeleton (Next.js PWA, MediaPipe in a worker, Claude structured outputs, Nano Banana 2.1, discriminated-union reducer, Zod 4, no event bus, Langfuse with inputs off); scoring separates them on the choices where they diverge: when the loop first works end to end on a phone, which data path faces travel, where ranking runs, and whether the plan's own stack can carry its own pipeline.
Recommended base: MVP-first (Plan A) as the base document, with Plan C's data path swapped in as non-negotiable grafts rather than options: Vertex with disableCache and region pinning from Phase 0 instead of the Gemini Developer API, OpenAI as a consent-listed fallback only, the on-device SSIM/landmark pre-gate plus union-mask paste-back in place of the Haiku-only face check, and the 'R2 keys, never bytes' rule the moment a durable runner is introduced. Plan A wins as the base because its two-week walking skeleton, single-language runtime, typed config with shutdown tests and lean async story are what a solo developer with AI assistants will actually ship and keep correct, and because C's deltas are provider and mask choices that drop into A's phases without restructuring them. Plan B is the source for the Phase 2 harness (thresholds, textured-hair subset, harness-delta PRs, taxonomy and schema constraints) but should not be the base: its ranking gate and job runner have no home in its own stack and its bake-off spends friends' faces on vendors whose terms nobody has read.

- MVP-first: feas 8.5 value 8 priv 7 cost 8 tech 7.5 honest 8 TOTAL 47
  Walking skeleton in two weeks (consent, HEIC-first decode, one Claude analyze, one NB 2.1 render, IndexedDB) is exactly how a consumer AI loop gets de-risked; everything stays TypeScript at runtime, so an AI assistant can hold the whole system in one language with a typed config module, a shutdown-date test and a schema test that forbids image bytes. Cost model checks arithmetically and is conservative ($0.08 analysis line is above its own stated Sonnet+Haiku figures). Loses points for picking the Gemini Developer API paid tier (55-day abuse logs, no region pin) over Vertex when the owner's stated default is 'most private that works' and the gap-fill shows Vertex costs ~1 day plus 10%; for a synchronous Promise.all route handler that will lose paid renders when mobile Safari backgrounds the tab and whose 60 s ceiling is itself unverified; for paste-back described against the original hair+forehead mask (clips any style longer than the current one) rather than the dilated union with the new-hair mask; for dropping the multiclass segmenter the prototype showed is needed for grey hair on light backgrounds (hair-segmenter IoU 0.36); and for repeating the 'cheapest frontier tier' superlative the gap-fill retracted. Bake-off after shipping is defensible for an MVP but means paste-back and refine tuning may be redone if the renderer changes.
- Quality-first: feas 5.5 value 8 priv 6 cost 7 tech 6.5 honest 7 TOTAL 40
  The eval design is the best of the three (golden set with hashes, textured-hair subset with its own thresholds, union masks, bald-first candidate, prompt change = PR with harness deltas, Claude structured-output constraints spelled out, non-commercial licences isolated by ADR). But the plan's own stack cannot run its own pipeline: the runtime ranking gate needs ArcFace, LPIPS/DreamSim and a face parser, none of which have a serving home in a TypeScript + Vercel + Trigger.dev design, and the ArcFace step computes a face template server-side (the very 'scan of face geometry' BIPA names) with 'per-person calibrated floors' that cannot exist for a new user with one selfie. Trigger.dev fan-out is declared from day one while the default path 'holds images in memory and returns results to the device'; nothing says how bytes reach the worker, and the two honest options (payload storage at a third party, or a bucket) both contradict the stated posture. The Phase 1 bake-off ships friends' faces to Seedream (SG/MY, terms unreachable), Krea 3 and Ideogram 4.5 (data terms never researched) and YouCam (30-day retention) without weighing that cost or naming a separate research consent. Timeline: five weeks of Python harness and an eight-config bake-off including a Klein self-host trial, then a four-week vertical slice that covers what Plan A spreads over nine weeks. Cost model is arithmetically correct but omits the +10% Vertex surcharge it chose and the second call in every bald-first chain. 'Refuse recognisable public figures up front' is unimplementable without identification, which the AUP forbids.
- Privacy-first: feas 7 value 7 priv 8.5 cost 8 tech 8 honest 7.5 TOTAL 46
  The most internally coherent data path: Vertex with disableCache and region pinning requested in Phase 0, no OpenAI by default, a per-user provider allowlist that the fallback chain respects, an on-device candidate pre-gate (outside-hair-mask SSIM plus landmark displacement) that keeps non-commercial recognizers out of production entirely, a HairEditProvider whose capability flags are enforced in-house because the AI SDK does not reject unsupported inputs, a clean SSE-first then runner-plus-R2-keys evolution, and the only plan that dates the AUP consent carve-out to the 2026-11-12 revision and schedules a recheck. Cost model is the only one that applies the Vertex surcharge and it states plainly that self-hosted Klein is a privacy premium, not a saving, at ten users. Deductions: the thesis says 'keep nothing server-side' and 'the app itself never stores a face' while the architecture keeps a transient crop in R2 for up to 24 h (correct engineering, overstated copy); Haiku 5.5 as the default analyst is a false economy when analysis is under 3% of session cost and the prototype only exercised Opus/Sonnet-class reasoning; fal-hosted FLUX 3 is the default refusal fallback without checking whether fal routes it to BFL as a partner API (fal's terms say partner models receive Client Content) or whether fal's DPA exists for self-serve, and its price was not found; with no OpenAI fallback a benign-selfie refusal dead-ends until Klein exists; friends features slip to Phase 3 and user-picked undertone is missing; the Phase 2 exit bar of $0.60/session is below the plan's own $0.62-0.74 arithmetic; Inngest's 5 concurrent steps will queue a demo evening.

Best ideas to graft:
- (MVP-first) Phase 0 walking skeleton in two weeks: consent, HEIC-first decode, one Claude analyze, one Nano Banana render, result in IndexedDB, three friends on real iPhones before any ranking, catalog or harness work. — De-risks the entire pipe (Safari 27 HEIC, canvas caps, Vertex auth, refusal shape, real latency, real cost) in the place where unknowns are largest; every other plan discovers these in week 5-9.
- (MVP-first) One typed config module for model ids, prices and deprecation dates with a unit test that fails once a configured model's shutdown date has passed; plus a schema-level test that no log, event, trace or DB row type can carry image bytes. — Cheap, deterministic guardrails an AI assistant cannot forget; gpt-image-1 dies in three days and 1.5/mini on 2026-12-01.
- (MVP-first) User-picked inputs for curl pattern (1A-4C picture chooser), stretched length, undertone swatch and style lane, with suggestions in balance language and 'no change, that contradicts' as a legal refine answer. — Directly implements the face-analysis lane's findings (VLM under-differentiates coily hair, photo undertone unreliable, hard shape labels flip across crops) and the prototype's contradiction handling.
- (MVP-first) OpenAI gpt-image-2.5-sunburst wired as a named, consent-listed refusal fallback and A/B, never the default; demo-evening analysis of the $10-per-10-minute Gemini spend-rate limit and 20 IPM tiers. — Both Google and OpenAI classifiers block benign real-face edits; a second vendor with different failure modes is the only cure, and the spend-rate math is what keeps a group demo from stalling.
- (Quality-first) Harness thresholds and golden set as the gate for every provider or prompt change, with the textured-hair and grey-hair subsets reported separately and held to the same bars; a prompt change is a PR that shows harness deltas. — No independent identity benchmark exists for any 2026 editor and textured hair is the documented failure class; making prompt edits measurable is the single biggest lever for AI-assisted iteration.
- (Quality-first) Hair mask = dilated union of the 0.78 MB hair segmenter and the multiclass segmenter's hair class (run once on the still), used both for paste-back and for metrics; bald-first chain as one of the candidates when the target is shorter or tighter. — The prototype showed the hair segmenter alone fails on grey hair (IoU 0.36) and the multiclass is too slow per frame but fine once per still; bald-first has research ablations behind it.
- (Quality-first) Claude structured-output constraints written into the schema conventions (additionalProperties false, no numeric min/max, flat all-required objects, unknown/not_visible members, provenance per field) and a typed HairDescription seeded from the Hairmony taxonomy plus headcutter-style maintenance fields. — These are the exact constraints an assistant will trip on; a taxonomy-ID-keyed description is what makes suggestions cacheable, diffable and exportable to the stylist sheet.
- (Quality-first) Non-commercial assets (InsightFace, StyleGAN2 FFHQ weights, research datasets, BiSeNet parsers) isolated in the offline harness by ADR, with the wall recorded for any future monetization. — Keeps the runtime shippable and the licence boundary explicit instead of discovered later.
- (Privacy-first) Vertex AI as the Nano Banana path from day one: billing attached, project cacheConfig disableCache=true, region pinned, abuse-monitoring exception requested in Phase 0, consent copy written only after the primary retention page is read. — Contractual no-training, region pinning and a disable-able cache for about one day of setup and 10%; the owner's CLAUDE.md default is the most private option that works.
- (Privacy-first) On-device candidate pre-gate: outside-hair-mask SSIM floor and landmark-displacement floor against the original, hair-region change to reject no-ops, then composite original pixels back outside the dilated mask union, all in the browser. — Gives a usable identity and over-editing proxy without server-side ArcFace/LPIPS, keeps non-commercial models out of production, and keeps rendered faces from making extra vendor round-trips.
- (Privacy-first) Once a durable runner is introduced, event payloads carry R2 object keys with a 24 h lifecycle and delete-on-completion, never bytes; until then a single SSE route handler. HairEditProvider capability flags (maxReferenceImages, supportsMask, supportsBoxes, retentionClass, residency) enforced in-house. — The only coherent answer in the three plans to how a fan-out worker gets the photo; the AI SDK documents the flags but does not reject unsupported inputs.
- (Privacy-first) Per-user provider allowlist chosen at consent time, with the refusal fallback chain filtered by it; hair-only crop of any reference photo on device so a third party's face never uploads; 'quirk:' comment class for gotchas an assistant cannot infer (MediaPipe GPU shared-canvas rule, HEIC decode on Safari, iOS rotation); eslint ban on console.* in lib/privacy; AUP recheck dated 2026-11-12; explicit Klein promotion criteria (identity pass rate within 5 points, friend rating within 0.5). — Each is a small, concrete mechanism that turns a privacy or quality intention into something enforceable or measurable.

Flaws:
- [high] (Quality-first) Trigger.dev fan-out from day one while the default path 'holds images in memory and returns results to the device': no defined way for the photo to reach the worker. Payloads mean a third party stores faces (Trigger.dev retains run payloads for its log window); a bucket contradicts the stated posture. The plan is silent on which.
- [high] (Quality-first) Runtime ranking gate depends on ArcFace, LPIPS/DreamSim and a face parser with no serving home in the TypeScript + Vercel + Trigger.dev stack; ArcFace at runtime generates a face template server-side (a BIPA 'scan') while the plan treats it as an open decision elsewhere; 'per-person calibrated floors' cannot exist for a new user with a single selfie.
- [high] (Quality-first) Phase 1 bake-off sends friends' faces to Seedream 5.0 Pro (SG/MY processing, terms unreachable), Krea 3 and Ideogram 4.5 (data terms never researched) and YouCam (30-day retention) without weighing the privacy cost or naming a separate research consent that lists those vendors.
- [medium] (Quality-first) Timeline: five weeks of Python harness plus an eight-config bake-off that includes a self-hosted Klein trial, then a four-week vertical slice covering what Plan A spreads over nine weeks; friends see nothing working until roughly week 9.
- [medium] (Quality-first) Twelve renders per suggestion round (3 suggestions x 3 primary + 1 secondary) before the first result, plus ranking, on a $1.15 session; heavy for ten friends and the p95 < 60 s bar depends on Nano Banana latency that no source measured.
- [low] (Quality-first) Chooses Vertex but prices Nano Banana without the +10% non-global surcharge; bald-first candidates cost two calls, not one; 'idempotency keys on every provider call' is not something Gemini or OpenAI image endpoints accept (only the jobs table can enforce it).
- [medium] (Quality-first) 'Refuse minors and recognisable public figures up front' is unimplementable for public figures without identification, which Anthropic's AUP forbids and Claude refuses; 'the session-event log is the eval dataset' overstates when renders live only on devices; 'cheapest frontier price at 1K' repeats a superlative the gap-fill retracted; the AUP consent carve-out is cited without noting it only takes effect 2026-11-12.
- [medium] (MVP-first) Default renderer path is the Gemini Developer API paid tier (55-day abuse logs of the face, no region pinning, ZDR eligibility unknown) when Vertex offers contractual no-training, a disable-able cache and region pinning for roughly a day of setup; this contradicts the owner's 'most private option that still works' default without an engineering reason.
- [medium] (MVP-first) Synchronous route handlers with Promise.all for 2-3 candidates: mobile Safari backgrounding drops the connection and the user loses renders already paid for; the 60 s maxDuration and 4.5 MB body limit on Hobby are both flagged unverified in the gap-fill; the 'renders table + polling' mention does not say how a disconnected client recovers a finished render.
- [medium] (MVP-first) Pixel paste-back is described against 'the dilated hair+forehead mask' of the original, which clips any target style longer or wider than the current hair; it needs the union with a mask computed on the output. The plan also drops the multiclass segmenter, so grey or white hair on light backgrounds (hair-segmenter IoU 0.36 in the prototype) gets a bad mask for both paste-back and descriptors.
- [low] (MVP-first) Candidate ranking rests on a Haiku 'face-unchanged check' with no pixel or geometry proxy at runtime, and every candidate image is sent to Anthropic even though the consent copy describes Anthropic as receiving the photo 'for description'; the repeated 'cheapest frontier tier' claim contradicts the gap-fill correction; live-recolor '7-15 fps' stretches the measured 8-12 fps hair-mask rate.
- [low] (MVP-first) Bake-off lands in Phase 2 after friends are already on NB 2.1, so paste-back, judge rubric and refine chips may be tuned against a renderer the bake-off replaces; results only in IndexedDB are subject to Safari's storage eviction for non-installed sites (acknowledged only indirectly).
- [medium] (Privacy-first) Thesis says 'keep nothing server-side' and 'the app itself never stores a face' while the architecture keeps a transient crop in a Cloudflare R2 bucket for up to 24 h and the structured section claims 'no first-party face storage' in the same breath; Cloudflare, Vercel and the runner are not named as processors in the consent copy.
- [medium] (Privacy-first) Haiku 5.5 as the default analyst and stylist brain is a false economy: analysis is under 3% of session cost, the prototype only exercised Opus/Sonnet-class reasoning, and the quality of 1A-4C and face-shape JSON on Haiku is untested; privacy does not require it since Sonnet and Haiku share the same data terms.
- [medium] (Privacy-first) fal-hosted FLUX 3 Image is the default refusal fallback without checking whether fal routes it to BFL as a third-party partner API (fal's terms say partner models receive Client Content and BFL's training terms are unread), whether fal's DPA exists for self-serve accounts, or what it costs (price not found).
- [medium] (Privacy-first) No OpenAI fallback means a benign-selfie refusal by Google and fal dead-ends until Klein exists in Phase 3; the Vertex abuse-monitoring exception that the privacy promise depends on may not be granted to a hobby project; Klein cold starts, GPU ops and the Kommunity s.2(e) filtering duty are unmeasured and would land on a solo developer.
- [low] (Privacy-first) Friends voting and any sharing slip to Phase 3, user-picked undertone is absent, the Phase 2 exit bar of $0.60 per session sits below the plan's own $0.62-0.74 arithmetic, bald-first double calls are not costed, and Inngest's 5 concurrent steps will queue a ten-friend demo evening.
- [low] (all) Shared gaps: none costs the second call in a bald-first chain; none notes that a mask-constrained edit (OpenAI alpha mask, FLUX boxes) cannot grow hair outside the mask, so the mask must be extended beyond the current hair; none mentions Anthropic's 'flagged content retained up to 2 years' caveat in consent copy; 'C2PA re-signed after re-encode' is asserted by all three without naming signing infrastructure; Nano Banana per-image latency is unknown yet every plan sets a p95 bar on it.

## Lens: Product and UX lead: weighing the friends' experience end to end (capture, consent, suggestion quality, refine convergence, stylist handoff, textured-hair and men's coverage, social features) and whether the MVP is something ten friends would actually come back to. Cross-checked every plan against the brief at <scratchpad>/digest/brief.md (read in full) and the five gap-fill lanes.
Recommended base: MVP-first. From the product lens it is the only plan that puts the real loop in friends' hands in two weeks and iterates on their reactions, which the brief says is the only evidence that exists for 'does it keep my face'; it already has the stylist reply link, the friends poll and the typed refine loop. Its gaps are all graftable at low cost: take Privacy-first's Vertex cache-off path, getUserMedia viewfinder with file-input fallback, top-three rendered with progressive reveal, on-device pre-gate and paste-back, capability-flagged provider interface and quirk comments; take Quality-first's refine-with-accepted-reference rule, separate colour and beard passes, textured and men's harness subsets, and a bake-off trimmed to Nano Banana 2.1, Pro and Sunburst on friends' consented photos. Privacy-first ties on total and is the right base only if the owner ranks privacy mechanics above time-to-first-render; even then drop its R2 staging and vendor allowlist. Quality-first should not be the base: its harness and eval discipline are the best of the three and should be adopted wholesale, but its sequencing delays all product learning to week 9 and its runtime gate and bake-off breach its own privacy rules.
